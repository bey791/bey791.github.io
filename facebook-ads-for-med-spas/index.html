Facebook and Instagram ads for med spas

By Ahmad Khan, Founder. Published and updated November 30, 2026.

Be Leaded plans Facebook ads for med spas that want more booked consults from Facebook and Instagram. Each offer gets its own campaign. Every inquiry is sent to your front desk.

Book a 30-minute call

Thirty minutes on your locations, the consults you want to advertise and the way your desk books visits. No pitch deck.

Already advertising on Facebook or Instagram? You can request a free Meta ad account audit.

What the program includes

Your spa holds the ad account. You choose which consults to advertise and you decide what counts as a booked visit or an attended visit. A clinician or owner at the practice reviews and approves each ad before we send it to Meta. Be Leaded handles the marketing work. We are not a clinic. We offer no clinical advice.

This program is for a med spa that wants Facebook and Instagram campaigns run for it, rather than building creative or the account itself. We prepare the ads, stand up a form or a short page and pass every inquiry to your desk. Your desk makes the first outreach. We never phone or message the person who filled out the form. Booked consults and attended visits come from what your office writes down. They describe the period just ended. They are not a forecast.

  • Creative tied to the consults already on your menu, such as injectable visits, laser hair removal and skin or body consults
  • A form inside the ad or a short page, with one offer and one scheduling question on each campaign
  • A radius around each location, limited to adults 18 and older when the offer is a cosmetic procedure
  • Delivery into your desk or the software you already use, with alerts in those same tools
  • A log of booked consults and attended visits, taken from what your office writes down, covering only the period just ended
  • A weekly look at what ran, what to pause and the next test

Programs start from $2,000 a month plus ad spend. Ad spend is paid to Meta directly and isn't part of our fee. Work runs month to month with no long-term contract.

Campaigns by treatment type

Injectable consults stay in one campaign. Laser hair removal stays in another. Skin or body visits stay in a third. Each has its own images, its own form and its own score, because a person shopping for hair removal is not the same buyer as a person shopping for a skin consult. The audience is drawn from the area around each location and from the offer on the ad. The line names a service the spa already provides. It does not comment on the viewer's skin, body or looks.

Consult types we can advertise

Injectable consults are often the first offer a spa wants in market, because the desk already knows how to book that visit. The ad names the consult the practice already provides. It does not tell the viewer they need a treatment. It does not invent a discount the spa does not already offer.

Laser hair removal gets a campaign and a form of its own. Someone seeking that consult is a different buyer than someone booking a skin visit. The ad can name the service and the area of the body the spa actually treats. It does not pledge a finished result or a set number of sessions.

Skin consults and body consults follow the same pattern: their own campaign, a visit the spa actually offers and no claim of a finished look in the copy. If your menu also includes a membership or a package consult, that offer waits until the first campaign is booking and the desk can keep up.

Creative for each consult type

Each campaign needs images and a line of copy that match the consult on offer, not a generic spa montage. We plan stills or short clips of the treatment room, the front desk and the visit itself, using assets the practice already has or that we shoot with you. A clinician or owner at the practice reviews and approves every frame and every line before we send the ad.

We do not run before and after images or review quotes in this program. The ad names the consult and invites the person to book a visit. It does not talk to the viewer's appearance. It does not claim a result in a set time. If a photo would make a clinical claim, we leave it out and use a room or process shot instead.

Headline and body copy stay short. They name the spa, the consult and the area you serve. A live ad also carries the practice name and the contact details Meta asks for. We keep one offer on each ad, so the form and the creative stay aligned.

From the inquiry to a booked visit

Once the form is in, your front desk gets an alert by email, by text or inside the software you already use. Your desk makes the first outreach, puts the consult on the calendar and notes whether the person shows up. We never phone or message the person who filled out the form. This page does not set a clock for that first outreach, because attendance turns on how quickly your office gets back to each inquiry.

We watch four stages: the inquiry, the first outreach, a consult on the calendar and a visit that was actually attended. Speed on that first outreach is the main lever. Those stages come from what your office writes down. They describe the period just ended. They are not a forecast.

How to read cost per booked consult

Likes, comments and cheap clicks are a poor way to judge med spa Facebook ads. The useful reading is how many inquiries became a booked consult and how many of those consults were attended, as your office wrote them down. Cost per inquiry can look low while cost per booked consult is high, if the desk cannot reach people or the offer does not match what the spa actually books.

We go through those costs with you each week, offer by offer and location by location. We do not publish a typical cost on this page. We do not attach a promised rate to any consult type. The figures you see are the ones your office wrote down for the period just ended. They help you decide what to pause or test next. They are not a pledge of future results.

Form in the ad or a short page

A form that lives inside the Meta ad is shorter and keeps the person in the feed. A short page can explain the consult in more depth before they submit. We choose one with you on the 30-minute call, based on the offer and on how your desk wants inquiries to arrive.

The form asks for a name and a way to reach the person, plus one scheduling question such as which location or which consult they want. It does not collect symptoms, medical history or payment coverage. Clinical questions wait until your practice is already in touch.

Spas with more than one location

A spa with several sites needs a radius for each one, a booking path for each one and a settled rule for which desk takes the inquiry. The ad can name the site the person would actually visit. If the practice wants each site to handle its own callers, we do not dump every response into one shared inbox. We also keep two sites out of the same campaign when their offers or hours differ.

If the locations share a brand but have different hours, menus or people who approve ads, those differences go into the campaigns and into the booking path. They are not papered over. The weekly review is broken out by site and by offer, so a strong location does not mask a weak one. The work still opens with one offer and one area. More sites are added only after the desks are already keeping pace with incoming inquiries.

What we look at in the weekly review

Each week we sit down with a short scorecard, not a pile of vanity totals. For each live offer and each location we look at how many inquiries came in, how quickly the desk made first outreach, how many consults landed on the calendar and how many of those consults were attended, as your office wrote them down. We also review which ads were paused and what to test next.

Slow first outreach is usually the first problem to fix, since people who wait often never land on the calendar. When one offer is seating visits and another is not, we put more weight on the one that is working or we recast the weaker creative, form or area. When the desk is already full, we hold off on a new offer until the practice can take it. None of those readings is a pledge about next week. This page does not assign a figure or a rate to any of them.

What Meta restricts in these ads

Med spa ads have to sit inside Meta's published standards, including the personal attributes rule and the Health and Wellness standard, both linked under Sources. Those standards limit the way an ad may address the person who sees it, who may see an ad for a cosmetic procedure and which questions a form may include. That is why the copy names a service and stays silent on the viewer's skin, body or looks. Forms collect a name, a way to reach the person and one scheduling question. Clinical detail is kept out of audiences, pixels and conversion events.

Meta's Health and Wellness standard limits cosmetic-procedure ads to people 18 and older. When the offer is a cosmetic procedure, we turn on the adult setting. How a given offer is classified is a decision for your practice and for Meta's review. That standard also forbids copy that attacks a person's appearance. It forbids a claim of a fixed result in a fixed window, unless a qualifier is present, in a health or cosmetic context. We write campaigns to sit inside those published standards. Meta still reviews every ad. Approval is never certain.

If Facebook and Instagram should be bringing in the consult types already on your menu, schedule a conversation. We will go over your locations, the path from inquiry to a booked visit and who approves the ads.

When this work is a fit

A good fit if

  • Spas whose desk can get back to new inquiries the same day they come in
  • Teams that can share which consults landed on the calendar and which people showed up
  • Practices willing to have a clinician or owner approve the ads

A poor fit if

  • Offices that want ads sent to Meta without a practice review of the claims in the ad
  • Teams that cannot get back to new inquiries the same day they come in
  • Anyone asking for a pledged visit count or a fixed cost for each attended visit

Questions

Short answers about Meta campaigns for a med spa.

Do Facebook ads work for med spas?

They can bring in people who want a consult, if your desk can get back to the person the same day the form arrives. The market, the offer and the speed of that reply all shape what you see. We score the work on booked consults and attended visits as your office writes them down. Those figures describe the period just ended. They are not a forecast.

What is med spa Facebook ads used for?

Most programs advertise consults the spa already offers, such as injectable visits, laser hair removal or a skin or body consult. Each offer gets its own campaign, so the form, the creative and the score stay tied to that visit.

How does a med spa advertising agency run this work?

In this program we plan and build the Facebook and Instagram campaigns. Your desk books the consults. Your spa owns the ad account. A clinician or owner at the practice reviews and approves each ad before it goes to Meta. We never phone or message the person who filled out the form.

Do the ads say the viewer needs a treatment?

No. Meta does not allow copy that acts as if the advertiser already knows a private fact about the viewer, including a health or cosmetic concern. The line names the service being offered. It does not comment on the viewer's skin, body or looks.

What is the form allowed to ask?

The form collects a name and a way to reach the person, plus one scheduling question such as which location or which consult they want. It does not collect symptoms, medical history or payment coverage. Clinical questions wait until your practice is already in touch.

Who owns the Facebook account and how is the fee set?

Programs start from $2,000 a month plus ad spend. Ad spend is paid to Meta directly and isn't part of our fee. The fee for your program is set during the 30-minute call.
