Facebook and Meta ads agency run for you

By Ahmad Khan, Founder. Published and updated December 2, 2026.

Be Leaded is a Facebook and Meta ads agency that plans and runs Facebook and Instagram campaigns for you. We handle strategy, creative, audiences, setup, tracking, landing pages, lead handling and reporting. You keep the ad account. Your team receives every inquiry.

What a Facebook and Meta ads agency does

Hiring a Facebook and Meta ads agency means handing the planning and the day to day work on Facebook and Instagram to a firm, rather than building every campaign inside your own team. The useful output is not a rise in likes. It is a steady path from an ad someone actually stops on, to a form or a short page, to an inquiry your staff can act on.

Be Leaded runs that work for companies that already have an offer people can say yes to and a way to handle the replies. We write the plan, produce the ads, stand up the campaigns, connect the tracking and pass each inquiry to the place you name. Your staff still owns the conversation with the person who responded. We do not sit in your inbox and speak for you.

Teams look for this kind of help for a few ordinary reasons. The account may already be live and no one inside the company has time to test creative each week. The work may never have been turned on. In that case the first setup needs a clear structure before money is spent. Or the company already knows which markets it serves and wants Facebook and Instagram treated as a program rather than a side task. In each case the job is the same: keep the account tidy, keep the ads honest and keep the handoff to your team reliable.

This page is about Meta placements only. Facebook and Instagram share one ads system, one pixel and one set of campaign tools. That is why the title and the heading name both Facebook and Meta. If you also want ads on LinkedIn and X, that wider mix is described on our page for Meta, LinkedIn and X campaigns. The sections below stay on Facebook and Instagram, including our sector programs.

What the program includes

The program is the marketing work required to plan and run the campaigns. You keep the ad account. You choose the offers that go in market and you approve every ad before it is submitted. You also decide what a useful inquiry looks like and where that inquiry should arrive.

We do the production and the account work. That includes the plan, the creative, the audiences, the campaign build, the tracking, the page or form, the route into your team and the weekly write up of what ran. The list below is the scope of a typical engagement. The exact mix is confirmed on the 30-minute call, because a practice with one office and a brand with a product catalog do not need the same setup.

  • A written plan for the first campaigns, including the offer, the audience and the event you want to count
  • Creative and copy for Facebook and Instagram, prepared for your review before anything goes live
  • Audience setup, including people already on lists you are allowed to use, people who visited a property you control and new audiences built from those sources
  • Campaign setup in your ad account, with names, budgets and placements agreed in advance
  • Tracking through the Meta pixel and, where your site can support it, the Conversions API
  • A lead form inside the ad or a short landing page, with one offer on each campaign
  • Handoff of each inquiry to your staff or your CRM, with alerts in the tools you already use
  • A weekly report covering what ran, what we recommend pausing and the next test

Work runs month to month with no long-term contract. You can stop at the end of a month. The ad account stays yours the whole time, including after the work ends. This page does not publish a fee, because the scope changes with the number of offers, locations and catalogs in play. We talk through that on the call.

Strategy and account structure

A strategy on this program is a short written plan, not a slide deck. It names the offer that will run first, the people it should reach, the event that counts as success and the way inquiries will arrive at your team. Everything else in the account is built to support that plan.

We start with one offer, or a very small set, so the first campaigns can be read clearly. Mixing several offers in a single ad set makes it hard to see which one the audience actually wanted. The same is true of a budget that is split across too many unconnected campaigns on day one. The opening plan therefore keeps the structure tight: a prospecting campaign for people who do not yet know you, a way to reach people who already visited a page or opened a form and a clean naming system so the weekly review is readable.

Account structure also covers the boring parts that later save time. Campaigns and ad sets are named after the offer and the audience, not after a date or a nickname only one person understands. Placements are chosen on purpose rather than left on a default that may not fit the creative. Exclusions are set so existing customers are not shown an acquisition offer unless you want that. And the events the pixel fires are limited to the ones you actually use, so the system is not optimizing for a cheap click that never becomes an inquiry.

If you already have an account, the first step is to read it. We look at how campaigns are grouped, which ads are still delivering, whether tracking is complete and whether the offer in the ads still matches the offer on your page. The free Meta ad account audit is available when you want that reading before a program starts. Findings from an audit can feed the plan, but they do not replace your approval of the first ads.

Creative and offer testing

On Meta the ad does a large share of the targeting work. People scroll quickly. The image, the first line and the offer have to make sense on their own. That is why creative is treated as a core part of the program, not as decoration added at the end.

We plan stills or short clips around the offer that is actually in market. The assets can come from what you already have, from a simple shoot we plan with you or from a clear product or location shot that does not invent a scene. Copy stays short. It names your company, the offer and the next step. A live ad also shows your business name. Each ad holds a single offer so the destination matches what the person just saw.

Testing means changing one thing at a time when we can. A new first line, a new image or a different first question on the form each answers a single question. Ads that stop getting attention are replaced. Ads that keep earning inquiries stay in market until they fade. We do not rotate creative just to make the account look busy. We also do not claim a test will lift a number by a set amount. The weekly review is where we show what was tested and what we want to try next.

Some creative is out of bounds on this program. We do not invent discounts you do not already offer. We do not write lines that pretend we already know a private fact about the viewer. We do not paste review quotes or before and after images into an ad unless you have a clear, approved reason to use them and they pass your own review. You see every ad, including variations, before it is submitted.

Audiences

Audience work on Meta is a mix of who you already know, who has already shown interest and who looks like those people. The plan names which of those groups each campaign should reach and which groups should be kept out.

If you have a customer list or a list of current accounts, that file can be used only when you already have permission to use it for this purpose. We do not upload a list you cannot stand behind. When a list is in good order, it can help us reach similar people or exclude people who already bought, already booked or already donated, depending on the offer. Website visitors and people who opened a form but did not finish it can be reached again from properties you control.

New audiences are built from the offer and the geography you actually serve, not from a long stack of interest guesses. A tight radius around a local office is a different design from a state or national plan for a product that ships. We test a broader audience against a tighter one when the account has enough delivery to make that comparison readable. The goal is not to collect the largest audience. It is to reach people who can take the next step you named.

Exclusions matter as much as inclusions. A person who just became a customer should not keep seeing the same first time offer unless you chose that on purpose. A person who already booked a consult should not be pressed by the same form the next morning. Those rules are written into the plan so the account does not spend against people you already won and so your staff is not chasing duplicates.

Campaign setup

Setup is the work of turning the plan into live campaigns inside your ad account. Nothing is switched on until you have approved the creative, the destination and the path inquiries will take.

We build the campaigns in your Business Manager, under names that match the plan. Budgets are set at the level you agreed, with a clear split between finding new people and reaching people who already visited. Placements are chosen to fit the creative. A square or vertical image that was made for the feed should not be forced into a placement that crops the offer off the screen.

Each campaign holds one offer. If you later want a second offer in market, it gets its own campaign, its own creative and its own form or page. That is slower on day one and much easier to read on week three. It also keeps a person from submitting a form for one offer and landing in a process built for another.

Launch is small on purpose. The first days are for confirming that events fire, that inquiries arrive where you said they would and that the ads are delivering in the area or market you named. We do not scale a campaign in the first days just to spend a budget. If something in the setup is wrong, it is cheaper to see that on a small delivery than on a large one.

Tracking, the pixel and the Conversions API

Tracking tells Meta which events to learn from. It also tells you whether the ads are producing the inquiries you actually wanted. Without it, the system can optimize for cheap clicks or short video views that never become a form submit.

The Meta pixel is a piece of code on your site that records browser events, such as a page view or a completed form. We install it or review the one you already have, then check that the events you care about fire once, with the right names and not on pages where they do not belong. A pixel that double counts, or that fires a purchase event on a thank you page that anyone can refresh, will mislead both the ads system and the weekly report.

The Conversions API, often called CAPI, sends the same kinds of events from your server rather than only from the browser. That matters when browser tracking is blocked or incomplete. Where your site and your tools can support it, we set up server events and deduplicate them against the pixel so the same form submit is not counted twice. Where your site cannot support that yet, we say so on the call and work with the pixel you have, rather than pretending the setup is complete.

We also agree, in writing, which events are the ones that matter. For many programs that is a form submit. For some it is a booking your team records in its own system. Store sales, donations and signed cases, when they are in scope, come from what your own tools and staff record, not from a vanity number inside Ads Manager. The weekly report uses those agreed events for the period just ended. It is a record of what happened. It is not a forecast of what will happen next month.

Landing pages, forms and lead handling

The ad can only do so much. The person still has to complete a form or a short page. Someone on your side still has to pick up that inquiry. Lead handling is the part of the program that connects those two steps.

A form inside the ad is brief and leaves the person in Facebook or Instagram. A short page on your site can say more about the offer before they send their details. We choose with you on the call, looking at the offer and at the inbox or CRM where your staff wants new inquiries to land. Each campaign keeps one offer and one path, so the person is not asked to choose among several unrelated asks on the same screen.

The fields are limited to a name, a contact method and one routing question, such as a location, an offer or a preferred time. It does not ask for a medical history, an injury history or a payment file. Those conversations belong to your staff after they are already in touch. If a sector program has a tighter rule on what the form may ask, that rule lives on the sector page and we follow it there.

Once the form is submitted, the inquiry is sent to the inbox, the desk, the CRM or the intake tool you named. We configure alerts in the tools you already use. Your team makes the first reply. We do not call or text the person who submitted the inquiry, unless a separate follow up program has been agreed in writing for that account. Speed on that first reply is mostly in your hands. Inquiries that sit overnight are harder to convert into a booked conversation, which is why the handoff path is settled before launch, not after the first form arrives.

If you later want the same inquiry to update a custom audience, or to send a signed outcome back into Meta as an event, we can talk through that once the basic path is working. That kind of loop is useful only when your team is already recording outcomes in a system we can trust. It is not a day one requirement.

Reporting and the weekly review

Reporting on this program is a weekly look at what ran, what it cost in media, what events and inquiries arrived and what your team recorded after that. The aim is to decide what to pause, what to keep and what to test next.

A useful report is broken out by offer and, where it applies, by location or catalog. A strong offer should not hide a weak one. We include the media bill from Meta, the events the pixel and the Conversions API recorded, the inquiries your tools received and, when you share them, the downstream counts your team already keeps, such as booked conversations, orders, gifts or signed matters. Those downstream counts are yours. We do not invent them and we do not treat them as a forecast.

Engagement totals such as likes, comments and low cost clicks are a weak way to read the program. Cost per inquiry can also mislead if the inquiries are a poor match or if your team cannot reach the people who submitted the form. We would rather read a smaller number of inquiries that your staff can actually handle than a large number that sits untouched. The weekly review is the place to say the volume is already more than the desk can take, so the campaigns can be slowed or paused.

The review also covers creative and audience tests that finished plus the next test we recommend. You do not have to accept every recommendation. The account is yours. You can ask us to hold, cut spend or change the offer. The report describes the period just ended. It is not a scoreboard of future targets.

Who owns the ad account

You own the Business Manager, the ad account, the Page, the pixel and the catalog, if you have one. Be Leaded is added as a partner with the access needed to build and manage the campaigns. That access can be removed when the work ends.

This point gets missed in a surprising number of agency setups. If the ads run inside an account the firm owns, you may not be able to take the history, the audiences or the pixel with you later. We do not run your spend through an account you do not own. Partner access inside your Business Manager is the arrangement this program uses.

You also keep the creative files produced for the campaigns, along with the weekly reports. If you already have an account, we work in that account rather than opening a second one without a reason. If you do not have an account yet, we help you open one in your name before the first campaign is built.

Billing for media is paid by you to Meta. The media spend is not rolled into a mark up that hides the actual bill. How our fee is scoped is covered on the call, because it depends on the work above, not on a single figure published here.

Facebook, Instagram and the Meta name

People still search for a Facebook ads agency. They also search for a Meta ads agency. Both queries point at the same kind of work: paid campaigns on the placements Meta owns, most often Facebook and Instagram.

Meta is the company name. Facebook and Instagram are the apps where the ads appear. Messenger and the Audience Network can also carry placements, but this program is planned around Facebook and Instagram unless you ask for something else and it fits the offer. Creative is made so it can work in the feed and in the tighter formats Instagram uses, when both are in the plan.

We use both names on this page so the heading matches the way people ask for the work. The campaigns themselves are built in Meta Ads Manager. If your team still says Facebook ads in conversation, that is fine. The account, the pixel and the reporting all live in the same system either way.

This is also why a sector page may say Facebook ads in its title and Meta in the body, or the other way around. The dentists and personal injury pages lead with Facebook because that is how those searches are phrased. The distributors page leads with Meta. The work underneath is the same family: campaigns on Facebook and Instagram, run for you, with the account in your name.

Be Leaded has a dedicated Facebook and Instagram program for several markets. Each market has its own page, with the rules and the measures that apply there. The notes below are short summaries so you can find the right page. They are not a substitute for those pages. They do not add new claims.

Pharmaceutical brands

For pharmaceutical brand teams, Meta work is planned around the product, the audience the brand is allowed to reach and the review path the brand already uses. Ads go through that review before they go live. Comment threads are watched for possible adverse event reports, because a public reply can carry information the brand has to handle. Audiences may be professional or consumer, as the brand permits. The fuller program, including platforms beyond Facebook and Instagram, is on our page for Meta campaigns for pharmaceutical brands. This summary does not add medical claims, spend figures or a review timeline.

Dental practices

Dental offices use Facebook and Instagram to advertise consults they already provide, with each consult type in its own campaign so the creative and the destination stay aligned. The office holds the account. A dentist reviews every ad before it is sent to Meta. New inquiries go to the front desk. We stay out of that conversation. Booked and attended visits are read from what the office writes down for the period just ended. Reach stays around each office, with an adult only setting when the offer is cosmetic. We handle marketing only. The full program is on our page for Facebook ads for dental practices.

Personal injury firms

Injury firm campaigns on Facebook and Instagram are split by the matter types the firm already wants to take. A person looking for counsel lands on a form or a brief page, then intake at the firm receives the inquiry. The firm sets the bar for a qualified intake and for a signed matter. Counsel at the firm reviews every ad and page before launch. Reach is limited to the states and practice areas the firm names. Copy talks about the firm's services and stays silent on the viewer's condition. Be Leaded is not a law firm. Full detail is on our page for Facebook ads for personal injury firms.

Distributors and wholesalers

Distributor and wholesaler campaigns on Meta are usually built for dealer or reseller recruitment, new business accounts and later ads to people who already visited a page or opened a form. Lead forms can pass into the CRM the team already uses. A customer list, when the company is allowed to use it, can support lookalike audiences or can keep current accounts out of a first time offer. The distributor page also covers the follow up after the click, because a form that sits until morning is often a lost conversation. This general agency page still treats that first reply as your team's job unless a follow up program is agreed separately. Read the detail on our page for Meta ads for distributors and wholesalers.

Retailers

Retail campaigns may aim at store visits, online orders or both. Before any proposal we talk through which locations and products should carry the ads and what the team can actually measure. Product catalogs need a feed that stays in step with stock, with product identifiers that match the site. Sale messages have to match the offer that is really available. Online orders can be read with the Meta pixel and the Conversions API. Store sales, when they can be tied back at all, depend on what the point of sale system can share. The fuller discussion of feeds, promotions, click and collect and marketplace overlap is on our page for Facebook and Instagram ads for retailers.

CPG brands

For a CPG brand, the first question is where the sale happens: the brand's own checkout, retail distribution or both. Availability comes before spend. There is little point advertising a product a shopper cannot actually buy in the area or the channel the ad names. Product claims in the ads need support the brand already has. Measurement has to stay honest about what the pixel can see versus what a retailer later reports. Meta is the usual starting point on Facebook and Instagram. Other placements are discussed on the sector page when they are relevant. The full program is on our page for Meta ads for CPG brands.

Nonprofits

Nonprofit campaigns on Facebook and Instagram treat a one time gift and a monthly gift as different asks, because the message and the page behind each one are not the same. The donation page matters as much as the ad. We measure gifts the organization records, not likes or shares. Supporter lists are uploaded only when the organization already has permission to use them for this purpose. Cause ads can fall under Meta's social issue rules, so the organization should be ready for that review. The calendar follows the organization's own appeals, not a generic season we invent. The full program is on our page for Facebook and Instagram ads for nonprofits.

How we start

The work opens with a 30-minute call. If it looks like a fit, we gather access and assets, draft the first campaigns and wait for your approval before anything goes live. Launch timing depends on how long that review takes, so this page does not name a go live window.

The first conversation

On the call we cover your market, the offers you want in market, how inquiries should arrive and whether you already have a Meta ad account. We also talk through what your team can handle in a week, because a campaign that produces more inquiries than the desk can reach is not useful. If the work is a poor fit, we say that on the call.

Account and assets

You open or confirm the Business Manager and ad account in your name. We are added as a partner. You share the offers, the images or clips you already have, the page or form destination and any list you are allowed to use. If tracking is missing or incomplete, we plan that work before launch rather than bolting it on later.

Your sign off

You review the creative, the destination and the handoff path. Nothing is submitted to Meta until that sign off is in hand. If a sector program needs a named reviewer, such as a dentist, counsel or a brand review team, that person is the one who signs, not a stand in.

One offer first

The first live work is one offer, or a very small set, in the market you named. More offers or more locations are added after the handoff is working and your team can keep up. This keeps the first weekly reports readable.

Each week's review

Once the campaigns are live, we meet the weekly cadence described above. You get a written look at what ran and a recommendation for the next test. You can ask us to hold or to change direction at any of those reviews.

When the program is a match

The program fits a company that has an offer people can understand on a phone screen, a way to handle inquiries and a willingness to keep the ad account in its own name. It is a poor fit when those pieces are missing, or when the brief is really a request for a set number of leads as a condition of starting.

A match if

  • You want Facebook and Instagram campaigns run for you, rather than a training session on Ads Manager
  • You can name the offer that should run first and you can approve creative in a reasonable time
  • Someone on your side can reply to new inquiries, in a desk, a CRM or an intake queue
  • You are willing to hold the ad account, the Page and the pixel in your own Business Manager
  • You want the weekly reading to be inquiries and the outcomes your team records, not likes or cheap clicks

Not a match if

  • You need a firm to run ads inside an account you will not own
  • You want a fixed number of leads, sales or appointments as a condition of starting
  • No one on your side can take the inquiries once they arrive
  • The brief is mainly organic social posting, influencer work or a site redesign
  • You want this page to stand in for a sector program that already has tighter rules, such as pharma, dental or personal injury work

If you already have ads running and mainly want a reading of the account, start with the free Meta ad account audit. If you want LinkedIn and X in the same program as Facebook and Instagram, use the page for Meta, LinkedIn and X campaigns rather than stretching this Meta only scope.

Questions

Short answers about hiring a Facebook and Meta ads agency and about how Be Leaded runs the work.

What does a Facebook ads agency do?

A Facebook ads agency plans and runs advertising on Facebook and Instagram, which now sit under Meta. At Be Leaded that means strategy, creative, audiences, campaign setup, tracking, landing pages, lead handling and a weekly review. You keep the ad account. Your team receives the inquiries and decides what to do with each one.

Is a Meta ads agency the same thing as a Facebook ads agency?

People use both names for the same work. Meta is the company. Facebook and Instagram are the placements where the ads appear. When Be Leaded acts as a Meta ads agency, campaigns are planned for both placements, not Facebook alone. The offer, the audience and the page or form stay aligned across them.

Who owns the ad account?

You do. The Business Manager, the ad account, the Page and the pixel stay in your name. Be Leaded is added as a partner on that account so we can build and manage the campaigns. If the engagement ends, the account, the history and the creative files stay with you.

What is included when the work is run for you?

The program covers strategy, creative, audiences, campaign setup, tracking (including the Meta pixel and the Conversions API where your site allows it), landing pages or lead forms, the handoff of each inquiry to your team and a weekly report. You approve the ads before they go live. The scope is agreed on the call, because it depends on the market and the offers you want in market.

How do you track what the ads produce?

We install or review the Meta pixel and, where your site can support it, the Conversions API so browser events and server events can be reconciled. We agree with you what a useful event is, such as a form submit or a booking your team records. Weekly reports describe what ran and what those events show for the period just ended. They are a record, not a forecast.

Do you run Instagram ads as well as Facebook ads?

Yes. The campaigns are planned as Meta campaigns, so Instagram is in scope along with Facebook. We choose placements with you based on the offer and the people you want to reach. Creative is written so it can work in both places when that is the plan.

How is this different from a program that also includes LinkedIn and X?

This page is only about Facebook and Instagram campaigns run for you. Some teams also want ads on LinkedIn and X. That wider mix lives on a separate Be Leaded page covering Meta, LinkedIn and X. The sector pages linked above stay on the Meta work for one market.

How do we get started?

Book a 30-minute call. We talk through your market, the offers you want in market, how inquiries should arrive and whether you already have a Meta ad account. If it looks like a fit, we gather assets, add partner access on your account, draft the first campaigns and wait for your approval before anything goes live.
