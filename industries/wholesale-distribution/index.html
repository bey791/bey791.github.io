Wholesale distribution

Lead generation for wholesale distributors

From the Be Leaded team. November 14, 2026.

Wholesale distribution lead generation, on this page, means outbound that opens conversations with buyers at the trade accounts you already care about.

You name the territory, the category and the type of account. We research the people who buy, send mail and LinkedIn notes under your name and place a meeting on your calendar when a buyer asks for one.

The work sits beside your reps. We do not take over the account or close the order. Paid social for the same audience is covered on Meta ads for distributors.

What this page covers

Distributors usually already have reps in a territory and a catalog that trade accounts recognize. The gap is a steady way to open new accounts without pulling those reps off current customers. This page is for that motion: a researched list, notes in your name and meetings handed back to the people who already sell.

It does not replace B2B lead generation services as the general outbound writeup, and it does not repeat the paid social offer. Read this URL if you sell through wholesale or distribution and you want buyers at named accounts reached directly.

Why new trade accounts are hard to open

Many wholesale books still grow from referrals, repeat orders and a few events each year. Those paths matter. They also have a ceiling. A buyer who already has a supplier will not change course because a catalog arrived unasked. The risk sits in stock, lead times, terms and the work of switching a vendor that already ships.

A show can start a useful conversation, then the follow-up sits behind current orders. Reps who know the territory are often already full with accounts they must serve. The open question is how to reach purchasing and operations at the next set of trade accounts, in your name, without pulling those reps off the book they already run.

This page is for that gap. It is not a claim that referrals should stop, and it is not a substitute for the relationships your team already has.

Reaching dormant and ERP accounts first

Before a new universe is built, many distributors already hold names that have gone quiet: accounts that once ordered, quotes that never converted and records sitting in the ERP with a last order date that has slipped. Manufacturer-forwarded introductions can sit in the same pile. Those names are often a better starting point than a cold list, because the account already knows the catalog or has at least seen a quote.

Reactivation is still outbound. The note should say why you are writing now, name the category you can still serve and invite a conversation if the account is buying again. It should not pretend a relationship is current when the file has gone cold, and it should not treat an old order as a reason to skip the brief. You decide which dormant records are in bounds and which must stay out, including any account a current rep already covers.

This work does not replace new-name outreach. It sits in front of it when the file already has a usable history. If the dormant set is thin, the researched list of new trade accounts is still the main path.

Who we reach at a trade account

You set the universe before research starts: geography, category, account type and any names to leave out. The people we look for are the ones who can start a vendor conversation, not a general inbox on the website.

  • Purchasing leads who review new vendors and replenish stock.
  • Operations or warehouse leads who feel lead times, fill rates and substitutions.
  • Category or branch buyers who own a set of products or a location.

A title is not enough on its own. In some books the buyer is a contractor, a store owner or a branch manager rather than a corporate purchasing desk. You name that pattern in the brief so the research follows the way your accounts actually buy. Each contact is checked against your rules before a message is sent.

Qualifying for a repeating account

A wholesale book grows from accounts that replenish, not from a single bulk drop that never repeats. Before a name goes on the list, it helps to ask whether that buyer could become a regular customer in your territory and category: do they already buy the kind of goods you stock, do they have a reason to need ongoing supply and is the account large enough to be worth a first conversation?

A one-off project purchase can still be a useful meeting. It should not be the only filter. You set what counts as worth a conversation for your book, including any account types that buy once and disappear. We use those rules when the list is built and when a reply is reviewed. We do not decide for you whether an account should be opened, and we do not score a buyer on a published model of future orders.

The meeting itself is still only a conversation. Credit, terms and whether the account is worth stocking stay with your team after the call.

How the outreach sits beside your reps

Your reps keep the relationship and the order. Our work stops when a qualified buyer is on the calendar. We do not quote pricing, write terms or walk a first purchase through your counter.

That split matters in wholesale, because a new account still has to be credit-checked, assigned and served. The meeting is the handoff. After that, your team decides whether the account is a fit and how to sell it.

How a program starts

The first step is a short call about the territory, the category and the account types you already sell into. You also set exclusions and what counts as a conversation worth booking. Research follows that brief. You see the list and the copy before anything is sent.

Outreach then runs on mail and LinkedIn under your name, at a careful pace. Replies are read. A conversation is booked onto your calendar only when a buyer asks to talk and matches the rules you set. Your reps take that conversation. We do not quote, credit-check or close.

The first meeting is a conversation with a buyer who asked to talk, not a close and not a quote. Your team uses it to learn how the account buys, whether the catalog fits and whether a next step is worth taking. We set the time and then step back.

You get a weekly recap of who was reached, who replied and which meetings are set. We do not quote a start date in advance, because setup waits on your approvals and on how large a universe you want reached. If the motion is not a fit, the call is the place to say so.

What the program includes

The outbound program covers the stretch from a brief you approve to a first conversation with a buyer. Data tools and inboxes do not have to come from your side to begin.

  • A list built to the territory, category and account rules you sign off, verified before anything is written.
  • Mail and LinkedIn in your name and voice, with each draft approved before it goes. See the cold email program and LinkedIn lead generation pages for how those channels are run.
  • Each reply is read, with appointment setting only when the buyer asked to talk and matches the brief.
  • Phone is included only if you ask for it. The cold calling services page describes that add-on.

Each week you get a short recap of contacts reached, replies and meetings booked. We do not quote a start date in advance, because setup waits on your approvals and on how large a universe you want reached.

The contact file and every reply thread stay yours. Outreach does not leave from your corporate domain.

What to measure after a meeting

Meetings booked are a useful activity mark, but they are not the end of the story for a distributor. After your reps take the call, the questions that matter are whether a new account was opened, whether a first order was placed and whether that account ordered again. Those marks sit on your side, in the ERP and in the book your reps already run.

We can report who was reached, who replied and which conversations were set. We cannot report fill, margin or reorder from our side, because we do not take the order. If you want those marks reviewed on a call, bring what your system already shows. We will not invent a conversion rate, a cost per inquiry or a typical reorder window, and we will not treat a booked meeting as a sale.

The first 90 days, written as phases

Some teams like to plan the first stretch of work as a set of phases. Ninety days is a familiar planning frame for that conversation. It is not a deadline, a launch clock or a promise that accounts will open or that a first order will land inside that window. Setup still waits on your approvals and on the size of the file you want reached.

Phase one is the brief and the file: territory, category, account types, exclusions and the dormant or ERP names you want included, plus the copy you will sign off. Nothing is sent until that is done.

Phase two is outbound under your name, on mail and LinkedIn, at a careful pace, with replies read and conversations booked only when a buyer asks to talk and matches your rules. Your reps take those conversations as they are set.

Phase three is a review of what the work produced: which accounts were reached, which meetings happened and, from your own records, whether any first orders or repeat purchases followed. That review is how you decide whether to keep the motion going for another month. It is not a score against a promised target.

How this differs from Meta ads

The Meta ads for distributors page is for paid campaigns on Facebook and Instagram: ads, forms and follow up after someone inquires. This page is for outbound to buyers you already want, whether or not they have raised a hand.

Keep Meta language on that URL. If you already run ads and also want a direct-to-buyer lane, the two can sit side by side. The lists stay separate.

Your catalog, product pages and any paid search work still sit with your team. This program does not build a website, run a customer portal or place search ads.

When this is a fit

The work suits a wholesale or distribution firm with a clear catalog, a defined territory and people ready to take a first conversation with a new account. It also suits teams whose reps are tied up with current customers and still want new trade accounts opening in the same footprint.

Skip this path if you only want paid social, if you need someone else to price the order or if no one on your team can take the meeting. Skip it if you need us to commit to a number of new accounts or to a first order. We do not publish those promises here.

Fees follow the published month to month lead generation terms. No 3-month commitment, pay month by month. You can leave when the current month has been completed.

If a territory and a category are already on paper, book a call. Bring the account types you already sell into.

Questions distributors ask

What does wholesale distribution lead generation mean here?

It means finding the right buyers at trade accounts that fit your territory and category, writing to them under your name and booking a conversation when someone asks to talk. It is not a list of inbound form fills, and it is not a promise that an account will place an order.

Is this the same as your Meta ads page for distributors?

No. The Meta ads page covers paid social campaigns, forms and follow up after an inquiry. This page covers outbound mail and LinkedIn to buyers you choose in advance. Some firms run both. They stay as separate programs, and the two files are not mixed.

Who do you contact at a trade account?

The people who can open a vendor conversation: purchasing leads, operations or warehouse leads and category or branch buyers, depending on how that account is organized. You set the rules before any note is sent.

Whose name is on the notes?

Yours. Domains are set up to sit with your brand, copy is written in your voice, and nothing goes out until you have signed it off. Booked conversations land on your calendar.

Is calling part of the program?

The default mix is mail plus LinkedIn. Phone is included only if you ask for it. Channel terms are written on the terms page for month to month work and on the cold calling services page.

Can we stop after one month?

Yes. Those terms appear on our month to month lead generation page: No 3-month commitment, pay month by month. You can leave when the current month has been completed. The contact file and every reply thread stay yours.

How does a program start?

With a short call on territory, category, account types and what you will treat as a conversation worth booking. Research and copy come next, and nothing is sent until you have signed off. Meetings are booked only when a buyer asks to talk and matches the brief.

Do you start with accounts already in our system?

If you want that path, yes. Dormant ERP records, quiet accounts, unused quotes and manufacturer introductions you already hold can be the first file we work from. You choose which names are in bounds, including any account a current rep already covers. New-name research still follows when that file is thin or when you want both.

What should we track besides booked conversations?

After your reps take the call, the marks that matter on your side are whether an account was opened, whether a first order was placed and whether that account ordered again. We can report who was reached, who replied and which conversations were set. We do not invent a conversion rate or treat a meeting as a sale.

Do you promise results inside 90 days?

No. Ninety days is only a planning frame for talking through phases of brief, outreach and review. It is not a deadline and not a promise that accounts will open or that a first order will land in that window. Setup still waits on your approvals.
