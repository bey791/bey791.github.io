How we compared

Cold email agencies fail in a few predictable ways: a burned domain, generic copy, replies that sit for days, and a meeting nobody agreed to call qualified. We checked each company against six things a buyer can confirm before signing. The order below follows the job, not a score.

  1. Domains and deliverability. Does cold email go out on separate, authenticated sending domains, or does your company domain carry the volume? You can check SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX with the inbox setup checker.
  2. Who writes the copy, and who approves it. Does someone write to the account, and do you sign off before anything sends?
  3. Reply handling. Who answers a positive reply, how fast, and who puts the time on your calendar?
  4. Meeting definition in writing. Does the contract name the title, the company, the problem, and who owns a no show?
  5. A price you can open. Is there a dollar figure on the vendor's own site? If the site has no dollar, the cell says pricing on request.
  6. Contract and channels. Month to month, a pilot, a yearly tier, or a four week cycle. Email, LinkedIn, calling, or a mix.

Belkins: best for packaged mid market volume

Best for: packaged mid market volume, when you want a yearly appointment tier and the budget for that package.

The appointment setting page prints an average starter price from $5,000. That card also names three outreach channels and a monthly lead volume of 1,500. Structured data on the same page sets a minimum price of $8,000, so confirm which number applies. The cold email page repeats the average starter price from $5,000. On the pricing page, retainers are sold as yearly appointment tiers, and that page prints no dollar amount. That pricing page also lists paid advertising, account based marketing, messenger, cold calling, LinkedIn, and cold email.

Domains: the cold email page says campaigns run on dedicated, warmed domains and mailboxes, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and that domain health is monitored. Reply handling: that page says SDRs review and respond to every message within 24 hours. The appointment setting page says the SDR shares a calendar link after a yes, then sends reminders and reschedules if needed. Ask how the contract defines a qualified appointment and who owns a no show.

Sources, checked October 17, 2026: belkins.io/appointment-setting, belkins.io/pricing, belkins.io/cold-email-services.

CIENCE: best for a GTM system plus SDR seats

Best for: buyers who start with CIENCE's go to market system, plus the platform, and can add SDR capacity later.

The pricing page lists a campaign team at $2,000 a month and graph8 at $499 a month. SDRs are extra and month to month: offshore from $1,750 and the US and Europe from $3,500 per SDR a month (cience.com/pricing, checked October 21, 2026). An example with the campaign team, graph8, and five offshore SDRs is $11,249 a month, plus a bonus when an SDR hits target. Setup is a one time fee with no dollar on the page, so that fee is pricing on request. The page says month to month, with no long contract.

Domains: setup includes email, calling, and LinkedIn infrastructure, with domains protected. The homepage says campaigns go out from protected domains. Reply handling: the homepage says the SDR reviews the opener, calls, and handles the reply, and that you can add or remove SDRs. Add the lines you would buy before you compare CIENCE with one retainer.

Sources, checked October 17, 2026: cience.com/pricing, cience.com.

OneAway: best for published pricing for SaaS

Best for: a B2B SaaS team that wants cold email, and maybe LinkedIn, with the price already on the site.

The homepage says OneAway runs cold email and LinkedIn outbound for B2B SaaS companies. Intro is $3,250 a month plus a $299 one time setup, up to 10,000 leads a month, cold email only, run by AI agents, with a 3 month commitment. Managed is $6,200 a month plus a $499 one time setup, up to 20,000 leads a month, cold email plus LinkedIn on 5 accounts, run by the team with agents, also a 3 month commitment. An appointment setter add on, for either package, has a person call every interested lead up to 6 times, at 2% of ACV per held meeting, minimum $150. Managed clients are capped on that page.

Domains: the cold email page says they build dedicated domains and warmed inboxes, with DNS authentication, so the primary domain never touches outbound. Reply handling: the homepage says every interested reply is handled, with up to 4 follow ups, and that both packages include a reply agent that books to your calendar. The 3 month commitment is the line to compare with a month to month retainer.

Sources, checked October 17, 2026: oneaway.io, oneaway.io/channels/cold-email.

Martal Group: best for a fractional sales team that runs discovery

Best for: a fractional team working onshore, which stays after the meeting for discovery and, on the higher tiers, for the sale.

The pricing page shows Customize your pricing, not a dollar retainer. The first tier, called Tier 1 outbound, bills one monthly flat fee. It starts with a pilot of 3 months, and after that the billing is a subscription each month. Tier 2 and Tier 3 add a sales commission on top of that monthly flat fee, and their pilot lasts 4 months. Scope is email, LinkedIn, and calls. Discovery is led by a North American team. Tier 3 also covers account management after the deal is signed. The price is a custom quote.

Domains: that page does not describe separate sending domains, so this page does not claim any. Reply handling: the team books appointments and leads discovery. It does not publish a reply time. If you only need a conversation on a calendar, the pilot is more sales process than that job.

Source, checked October 17, 2026: martal.ca/pricing.

Best for: done for you personalization with email plus LinkedIn and same day human replies. Programs start from $2,000 a month.

Leadium: best for a month to month multichannel retainer

Best for: month to month outbound across calling, email, and LinkedIn, with a published retainer.

The pricing page says contracts are month to month. Launch timelines there are 7 to 10 days for appointment setting, 1 to 3 days for lead generation, and 1 to 2 days for outbound consulting. Leadium does not guarantee appointment outcomes. The pricing page does not print the monthly dollar. Leadium's outbound guide does: cold calling only is $3,500 a month, and multichannel is $4,000 to $5,000 a month.

Cleverly: best for LinkedIn first on a small budget

Best for: a LinkedIn first test on a published plan.

The FAQ says lead generation plans range from $397 a month to $997 a month. The LinkedIn page shows $397, $697, and $997 a month, and says response handlers manage the inbox and book on the calendar. The FAQ also says cold email is pay per meeting ready lead, month to month, with no contract, and it does not list that price.

The cold email page shows Growth as a flat monthly retainer and Scale as pay per lead. Neither prints a dollar, so the cold email price is pricing on request. Both tiers list email inbox and domain setup. The page says month to month, no contracts, and that you are notified when a meeting ready lead comes in. It does not say the company domain stays off cold sending. Treat the printed dollars as LinkedIn plans.

Sources, checked October 17, 2026: cleverly.co/faq, Cleverly LinkedIn pricing, Cleverly cold email.

SalesRoads: best for a phone first calling floor

Best for: a dedicated outsourced SDR team where calling is the main channel.

The pricing page lists 1 to 6 SDRs. One SDR is $11,950 per four weeks, two are $16,750, and three are $23,500. A program launches in 15 business days. There is no long term commitment, and you can cancel any time. The playbook includes call approaches and email cadences.

Domains: the outbound email page says SalesRoads warms domains, rotates sending domains, uses a dedicated IP per client, and monitors sender reputation. Reply handling: that page says responses are analyzed to refine messaging. It does not publish a clock time for a human reply. One SDR at $11,950 per four weeks is a calling team price, not a monthly cold email retainer.

Sources, checked October 17, 2026: salesroads.com/pricing, SalesRoads outbound email.

SalesHive: best for month to month with SDRs

Best for: a flat monthly fee that includes SDRs, when you can accept a custom quote instead of a price on the page.

The pricing page says the managed program is one flat monthly fee for the SDR team, a strategist, the platform, and data. Starter, Growth, and Crush are custom quoted. The rate depends on US based or offshore SDRs, calling only or calling plus email, and team size. There is no setup fee. You owe nothing until you approve launch. Month to month cancellation needs written notice, and billing ends at the end of the next billing month. Annual plans cost less per month and run longer. The homepage says there is no long term lock in and that meetings are not guaranteed. No dollar is printed, so the price is pricing on request.

Domains: the pricing page does not describe separate sending domains, so this page does not claim any. Reply handling: the managed fee includes reply handling, and the homepage describes a smart inbox for reply triage. Neither page publishes a same day rule.

Sources, checked October 17, 2026: saleshive.com/pricing, saleshive.com.

SalesBread: best for researched lines on a month to month service

Best for: researched lines on the client's LinkedIn account, month to month. The homepage does not say hand written. It does say researchers write the lines and that the company does not use AI.

The homepage says the service is month to month. Researchers look up the prospect, including a podcast, a talk, or the company bio, and write the line. SalesBread monitors the inbox and emails the client when someone at the right company and title replies with interest. The same site says email is used with LinkedIn when the job is booking, and that interested leads get a fast response. A wait list is described as 3 to 5 weeks. No dollar appears on the homepage or the done for you page we opened, so the price is pricing on request.

Domains: those pages do not describe separate sending domains, so this page does not claim any. Reply handling is the inbox watch plus an email when a lead is ready. There is no same day clock on the page.

Reply handling and what counts as a meeting

Two lines decide whether two agencies are selling the same thing: who handles a reply, and what a qualified meeting means. Ask every vendor to put both in writing before you sign.

Who reads a positive reply, who books the time, and how fast that happens. What title, company, and problem count as qualified, and who owns a no-show. If those lines are missing, the fee is not comparable.

Be Leaded's published line on this site is: a person answers replies the same business day. Scope is on the pricing page. The appointment setting page is where a qualified meeting is defined in writing. For a side by side that includes Belkins, see Belkins alternatives.
