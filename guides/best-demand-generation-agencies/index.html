The two jobs

People searching for the best demand generation agencies are usually mixing two purchases. One purchase is content and paid demand creation. The other is outbound that books calls. Shoppers also type best b2b demand generation agencies. That longer wording still splits into the same two purchases. Nine agencies follow, in the order of this page.

  • Refine Labs, when the pricing page prints paid media and a fuller demand service in dollars, for B2B SaaS companies at Series B and beyond.
  • Directive, when the demand generation page names content, paid media, SEO, and account based marketing, and prints no agency fee.
  • Powered by Search, when paid media, organic demand, and RevOps each show a monthly start on the pricing page.
  • Ironpaper, when the demand generation page describes advertising and content for B2B companies and prints no fee.
  • Be Leaded, publisher of this page, placed at number five.
  • Gripped, when the retainer is in pounds and the pricing page is titled for SaaS and tech. This is the same content and paid job as the first four.
  • Sopro, when the offer is managed outbound and the pricing page prints a start price and no minimum contract.
  • Callbox, when one campaign pod is priced as a quarterly estimate.
  • SalesRoads, when calling and email are priced as a four week block.

Software or services is only what each site prints. Refine Labs names B2B SaaS companies at Series B and beyond. Gripped titles its pricing page for SaaS and tech. Directive's menu lists Technology, Industrial, and Services. Powered by Search says the fit is a B2B company, and that a B2C or ecommerce brand is outside that fit. Ironpaper addresses B2B companies and does not split software from services. Sopro, Callbox, and SalesRoads do not print that split on the pages opened October 21, 2026.

If a page prints no dollar, the cell says no public price. Nothing in the table is filled from a roundup.

How the nine were chosen

A buyer can repeat the six checks. Ratings did not decide the order. Staff size did not decide the order.

  1. Which purchase the agency's own page describes: content and paid demand creation, or outbound that books calls.
  2. Whether a dollar or a pound figure is printed, and which unit that figure buys.
  3. The channels that page names.
  4. Any minimum, notice, or cancel line that page writes down.
  5. Whether the page says who holds the ad accounts, the audiences, and the pixels.
  6. Whether the page names a software buyer, a services firm, or neither.

Silence on their page stays silence here. No third party roundup was used as a source. The outside pages under Sources were opened on October 21, 2026.

Questions to ask before you sign

Put these to any firm before you sign, the publisher included. They cover pipeline and ad accounts.

  • What does the quote count as pipeline, and which stage has to be reached before it counts?
  • Which lines will the monthly report show for pipeline created, booked calls, cost per channel, and who holds the data?
  • Will the ad accounts, audiences, and pixels sit in your name, with the agency added as a partner?
  • If the work ends, do the contact list and the CRM history stay with you?
  • Is cold calling quoted as its own fee, or is it absent from the quote?
  • Which purchase is on the order form: content and paid demand creation, or outbound that books calls?
  • Does the dollar on the quote match a page that agency hosts, and which date did you open that page?
  • Does that agency's own page name a software buyer, a services firm, or neither?

What should a demand gen agency report each month?

Ask for four lines in the monthly report: pipeline created, booked calls, cost per channel, and who holds the data. Put the definition of pipeline in the same document as the quote, including which stage counts. This page does not add numbers of our own. How this publisher reports sits on the reporting section.

Who keeps the ad accounts, audiences and pixels when the contract ends?

Check before you sign. Some agencies run ads inside your own ad accounts, so the audiences and pixel history stay with you. Others run them in accounts the agency owns, and you may lose those audiences and the tracking history when you leave. Ask for the ad accounts and pixels to sit in your name, with the agency added as a partner. Where a listed agency states its policy on its own page, the entry quotes it with the check date.
