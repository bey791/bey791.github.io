A managed option: Be Leaded

Be Leaded is a B2B outbound agency, not a texting app. We run cold email and LinkedIn to start conversations, then use SMS through ClickSend once a buyer has replied and said yes to texts or opted in on a form. Our clients are B2B companies that sell by email first, want texting after the yes and would rather not staff a texting tool. Month to month, no long-term contract. If that sounds closer to your problem than a new platform, pick a time for a 30 minute call.

Quick answer

ClickSend is a capable general purpose business messaging service. People look for alternatives when their needs grow in one direction: reps want texting inside the CRM, marketers want richer list building tools, support wants a routed team inbox or developers want lower level control of numbers and delivery.

Each direction points to a different group of products. This guide sorts twelve of them by the need they answer, quotes only what each vendor publishes on its own site and leaves prices out because plans change too often to compare fairly.

One more honest point. Some teams searching for a replacement do not have a tooling problem. They have too few engaged buyers to text. If that is you, a new platform will not help and the section on managed outbound below is worth reading first.

What ClickSend does well

Any fair comparison starts with the product you are comparing against. According to its US site, ClickSend lets companies send SMS and MMS from a browser dashboard, from connected apps or from code, for messages inside the United States and abroad.

  • Marketing texts, broadcasts plus alerts and notifications from one account
  • MMS so messages can carry images or sound
  • Newer channels including RCS, Facebook Messenger and WhatsApp Business
  • An integration marketplace for connecting business apps without code
  • Support by chat at any hour, per the site

That breadth is the reason we use ClickSend ourselves for client programs. It covers the sending we need and connects to the rest of our stack. The alternatives below are not better or worse in general. They lean harder into one job.

Why teams look beyond ClickSend

In our conversations with B2B buyers, the reasons for shopping around tend to fall into a few patterns. None of them mean ClickSend failed. They mean the job changed.

  • Reps live in the CRM: a sales team wants every text logged on the HubSpot or Salesforce record without switching windows
  • Many people answer replies: support or success teams need assignment, routing and internal notes on each thread
  • Marketing wants list growth: popups, keywords and QR codes to collect opt ins at scale
  • Developers want control: direct number management, delivery receipts and SDKs for a product feature
  • Compliance needs a paper trail: a regulated team wants consent records and audit reports in one place
  • The real gap is pipeline: there are not enough engaged buyers to text in the first place

Name your pattern before you book a demo. It narrows twelve options down to three or four.

The twelve alternatives in detail

Each alternative here has an official site we opened and checked ourselves. Note: these alternatives appear A to Z by name. Their order is not a ranking. Every profile says what the vendor offers in its own words, when it may suit you better than ClickSend and what we would ask before signing.

EZ Texting

EZ Texting is built around SMS marketing, with mass sends and one to one conversations in the same account. Its homepage lists bulk texting, two way messaging, short codes and drag and drop workflows. Its own ClickSend comparison page points to list growth tools such as keywords and sign up forms.

  • Bulk sends and two way texting from a web app
  • Short code support for larger programs
  • Workflows built by dragging steps into place
  • Messages in six character sets, per the site
  • Opt in capture, STOP links and help with A2P registration

Consider it if: your main use is marketing texts to people who signed up, such as event or webinar audiences and you want collection tools built in.

Ask in the demo: how replies are assigned when several colleagues answer and which CRM fields sync both ways.

With cold email: EZ Texting fits audiences who signed up for something, like an event list. Keep cold prospect lists out of it and add contacts only once they opt in.

Heymarket

Heymarket presents itself as a shared inbox for business messaging. Its homepage covers SMS, email and messaging apps in one place, with team features and CRM connections for sales and service.

  • Shared inboxes so several people work the same threads
  • Salesforce and HubSpot integrations plus an API
  • Drip campaigns built from saved templates
  • Automations that fire on a keyword or a schedule
  • Roles, 10DLC support and opt in management

Consider it if: your team needs to hand threads between people without losing history and the CRM is where everyone works.

Ask in the demo: how a thread moves from a campaign reply to the right owner and how consent shows on the CRM record.

The site also states SOC 2 Type 2 and HIPAA readiness. Ask for current documents if either matters to you.

With cold email: when a buyer answers an email and agrees to texts, a rep can pick up the thread in Heymarket with the CRM history beside it. Assign threads by account owner so buyers hear from the person they met.

Plivo

Plivo offers an SMS API alongside marketing features. Its SMS page covers one time codes, alerts, promotions and conversational messages, plus tools for segmenting audiences and building journeys without code.

  • An API for transactional and conversational texts
  • Fraud prevention features for messaging traffic
  • Segmentation and customer journeys
  • A no code studio for building flows
  • Integrations named include Shopify, WooCommerce, Salesforce, HubSpot and Zoho

Consider it if: you want an API for product messages and a visual tool for marketing in the same account.

Ask in the demo: who on your side maintains the flows and how consent from your forms reaches the platform.

Plivo describes GDPR and TCPA compliance on its page. Confirm what that covers for your use.

With cold email: Plivo suits the product side of a B2B company more than the sales side. Pair it with a CRM based tool if reps also need to text buyers one to one.

Salesmsg

Salesmsg is aimed at revenue teams that want texting and calling inside the CRM. Its homepage highlights a two way HubSpot sync, a Salesforce integration and a shared inbox.

  • Texts and calls from inside HubSpot or Salesforce
  • Broadcasts, MMS and ringless voicemail
  • Scheduled messages and business hours settings
  • A power dialer for call heavy teams
  • Opt out handling, double opt in and 10DLC registration

Consider it if: reps are measured in the CRM and you want every touch logged there by default.

Ask in the demo: which CRM events can trigger a text and how you stop a trigger from firing for contacts without consent.

The site lists SOC 2 and offers a mobile app and a Chrome extension. It also names lead capture by web widget, QR code and keyword.

With cold email: tie the first text to a CRM stage that only a recorded yes can reach. That keeps automations from texting contacts who never agreed.

SimpleTexting

SimpleTexting describes itself as a texting service for small businesses. Its homepage mentions getting a new number or text enabling an existing landline, then growing a list and answering one to one.

  • Popups and keywords for collecting opt ins
  • Blasts plus two way threads with templates and auto replies
  • Scheduled messages
  • API access on every plan
  • Help with 10DLC registration

Consider it if: you want a straightforward app for a small team and a path to text from the number customers already know.

Ask in the demo: how the integrations it lists connect to your CRM and what its engineers build for you on request.

With cold email: use SimpleTexting for confirmations once a buyer has agreed, sent from a number they recognize. Its templates keep scheduling messages short and consistent.

SlickText

SlickText focuses on text marketing with strong list building. Its homepage describes several ways to collect subscribers plus segmentation, workflows and an inbox where teams can route messages.

  • Popups, web forms, tap to join, keywords and QR codes
  • Segments for sending to the right subset
  • Drag and drop automation workflows
  • An inbox with routing to team members plus tags
  • Use of an existing number or a short code

Consider it if: marketing owns texting and the priority is growing an opted in audience with clean segments.

Ask in the demo: how B2B contacts with several people per account are handled and how the HubSpot integration maps fields.

The site also names Shopify and Zapier integrations, onboarding with customer success managers and US based support.

With cold email: SlickText is strongest for marketing lists. For sales threads with individual buyers, check whether the inbox routing to team members gives each rep their own conversations.

Telnyx

Telnyx sells messaging as infrastructure for developers. Its SMS API page covers SMS and MMS through REST APIs with SDKs in five languages.

  • 10DLC, toll free, short code and alphanumeric sender ID
  • Queuing, delivery reports and automatic STOP handling
  • Hosted SMS on numbers you already own
  • 10DLC registration from the portal or through the API
  • Support around the clock and usage based pricing, per the page

Consider it if: your product sends texts and your developers want direct control of numbers and delivery data.

Ask in the demo: what your team must build for inboxes and consent records, since an API leaves those to you.

With cold email: an engineering team can build the exact trigger it wants, such as a text sent when a CRM deal changes stage after a reply. Plan who maintains that build.

Textline

Textline is business texting organized around teams. Its homepage describes a unified inbox for SMS, web chat and social, with departments that can each hold their own numbers.

  • Routing so threads reach the right person
  • Automations and internal notes
  • Team metrics
  • HubSpot and Zendesk integrations
  • Surveys and web chat that continues by text

Consider it if: support or success teams answer most of your texts and need structure more than campaigns.

Ask in the demo: how departments share contacts and how the integration with your help desk logs each thread.

The site states SOC 2 and HIPAA and offers registration guidance. Request documents if your buyers ask.

With cold email: Textline suits the phase after the first meeting, when several buyers send questions about access, documents or timing and each needs the right owner.

TextMagic

TextMagic covers business texting with built in compliance tooling. Its homepage separates operational messages from permission based ones and guides teams through registration.

  • Guided 10DLC and toll free registration
  • Consent forms with STOP and HELP replies handled for you
  • Setups described for the UK, Canada, Australia and Europe
  • SMS and email with a shared inbox
  • Zapier, an API and pay as you go billing

Consider it if: you text buyers in several countries and want one place to keep consent and opt outs consistent.

Ask in the demo: how consent captured outside the platform gets imported with its date and source.

The site names GDPR and SOC 2. Check the scope against your own obligations.

With cold email: TextMagic suits international buyers who agreed to texts, because its consent tooling is described market by market. Import consent dates along with the contacts.

TextUs

TextUs serves recruiting, HR, sales and customer service teams. Its homepage stresses connections to CRM and applicant tracking systems so texting happens next to existing records.

  • Salesforce, Greenhouse and HubSpot integrations
  • A number per person or one for the whole team
  • Apps for phones and a browser extension for Chrome
  • White label and OEM options
  • Opt out and consent tracking

Consider it if: one platform has to serve both a sales team and a recruiting team.

Ask in the demo: whether your buyers are outside the US, since the site says texting is US only and offers WhatsApp for international contacts.

The site describes a volume based pricing model. Ask how it applies to your expected use.

With cold email: the browser extension puts texting beside the CRM contact, which keeps the move from an email reply to a text in one place. Check the US only limit against your buyer list.

Twilio

Twilio offers messaging building blocks for companies that write their own software. Its messaging page describes APIs for programmable messaging, conversations and verification.

  • SMS, RCS, MMS, WhatsApp and Messenger from one platform
  • Trust Hub for registration and Lookup for number checks
  • Content Template Builder for message formats
  • A Salesforce app plus HubSpot connections through integrations or Zapier
  • Pay for what you use with no subscription, per the page

Consider it if: you have engineers and a product that needs texting woven into it.

Ask in the demo: who owns the registration, the inbox and the consent store, because you build those parts.

With cold email: Twilio lets developers wire texting into any flow, including a reply in your email tool. Budget for the inbox and consent store you will need to build around it.

Vonage

Vonage provides communications APIs including SMS. Its SMS API page covers the Messages API, MMS, private SMS and APIs for verification and reporting.

  • 10DLC, short codes and toll free numbers
  • Opt Out Assist for handling stop requests
  • Adaptive routing
  • A Global Compliance Engine plus Fraud Defender and Traffic Control
  • Reports API for delivery data

Consider it if: you send at scale across countries and want routing and fraud tools from the same provider as your voice or video APIs.

Ask in the demo: how much of the inbox and consent workflow you will build versus get out of the box.

With cold email: Vonage fits companies that already use its voice or video APIs and want messaging from the same provider. Sales texting still needs an inbox layer on top.

Which alternative suits which team

The same company can need different texting tools in different departments. Matching the product to the team that will use it every day avoids the most expensive mistake in this category: a tool nobody opens.

Sales and business development

Sellers want to text from the record they already have open. Salesmsg, Heymarket and TextUs describe this kind of CRM connection on their sites. Look for logging that needs no extra clicks and a clear way to see which contacts agreed to texts.

Marketing

Marketers care about growing an opted in audience and sending the right message to the right segment. EZ Texting, SimpleTexting and SlickText put list building tools front and center. Check how each one records the source of every sign up.

Support and customer success

These teams need routing, notes and a shared view of each conversation. Textline and Heymarket describe inbox features built for this. TextMagic also offers a shared inbox alongside its consent tooling.

Product and engineering

When texts are part of the product, such as sign in codes or account alerts, an API is the natural fit. Plivo, Telnyx, Twilio and Vonage all publish developer documentation. ClickSend has an API too, so compare the details rather than the category.

Recruiting

TextUs names recruiting and HR teams on its homepage and lists an applicant tracking integration. If your company hires often, check whether one account can serve sales and recruiting with separate numbers.

Testing an alternative with a two week pilot

Demos show the best case. A pilot shows how the tool behaves on your real Tuesday afternoon. Keep it small and specific.

  • Pick one use: for example meeting reminders for buyers who already agreed to texts
  • Pick one owner: a named person answers every reply during set hours
  • Pick three measures: show rate, time to reply and opt outs
  • Keep ClickSend running: for everything outside the pilot until you decide

At the end, compare the measures with the same period on ClickSend. If the new tool is not clearly easier for the owner to run, the switch is probably not worth the migration work.

Sender types you will see in every demo

Vendors use the same handful of terms for the numbers you text from. A quick translation makes comparisons easier.

  • Local ten digit numbers (10DLC): the usual choice for US business conversations, registered with a brand and a campaign description
  • Toll free numbers: recognizable as business lines, with a separate verification step
  • Short codes: five or six digit numbers for large programs, approved on their own timeline
  • Alphanumeric sender IDs: a name instead of a number, available in some countries outside the US
  • Text enabled landlines: your existing office number set up to send and receive texts

For B2B conversations, buyers tend to respond better to a number they can call back. Ask each vendor which types it supports, which registration it files for you and whether replies can ring through to a person.

What to tell your team before a switch

People who answer texts every day will notice a change before anyone else. A short brief avoids missed replies in the first week.

  • The date the new number or inbox goes live and what happens to old threads
  • Who owns which conversations and how handoffs work
  • The exact wording to use when someone asks to stop
  • Where to log a buyer's agreement to texts if it arrives by email or phone
  • Who to tell when something looks wrong

Ten minutes of explanation saves days of cleaning up duplicate contacts and confused buyers.

Reporting that shows whether the switch paid off

Most platforms report sends, deliveries and replies. Those numbers describe activity. To judge the switch, track outcomes your sales leader already cares about.

  • Meetings held as a share of meetings booked
  • Days from a positive reply to a booked meeting
  • Opt outs per hundred contacts texted
  • Weekly time your staff put into answering texts

Pull the same figures for the month before the move. If the new tool does not improve at least one without hurting the others, revisit the decision before the renewal date.

If you hire a service instead, ask these

  • Whose texting software will carry our messages and who files the number paperwork?
  • What counts as permission to text one of our buyers?
  • Do you ever text people who have not agreed? (The right answer is no.)
  • Who keeps the contact list and threads if we part ways?
  • Can we end the program after any month?

Our answers are ClickSend with numbers registered before launch, a recorded yes or a form opt in, never, you do and yes.

Inbound leads and texting after a form

Not every text starts with a cold email. Many B2B teams also get demo requests, webinar sign ups and pricing inquiries through their website. Those forms are the cleanest place to collect permission to text, as long as the checkbox is optional and the wording says what you will send.

Whatever tool you choose, connect the form directly to it so the opt in date and page travel with the contact. Then text within minutes while the request is fresh. A fast first reply to an inbound lead often matters more than which platform sends it.

Be Leaded handles both paths in client programs: buyers who reply to outbound and agree to texts and inbound leads who opt in on a form. Both enter ClickSend with their consent recorded.

  • What we never do: text bought lists or numbers scraped from the web
  • Numbers: registered for business texting before anything is sent

When staying with ClickSend makes sense

Switching costs time. Number registration, contact imports, new integrations and retraining all take effort. Staying put is often the sensible choice in these situations.

  • Your texting is mostly alerts and reminders and the dashboard covers it
  • You already use integrations from its marketplace that work
  • You send to several countries and like having one account for all of them
  • You want RCS, WhatsApp Business or Messenger from the same provider
  • The frustration is really about who answers replies, which no platform fixes

If two or more of these describe you, try fixing the workflow first. Assign an owner, set reply hours and connect the CRM before you move numbers.

How to switch without losing contacts or consent

If you do move, plan the change like a small project. The biggest risks are losing the consent history and a gap where messages stop.

  • Step 1: Export contacts with opt in dates, sources and opt out flags
  • Step 2: List every integration and automation that sends or receives texts
  • Step 3: Check with the new vendor whether your numbers can move or need new registration
  • Step 4: Register the brand and campaign with the new vendor before cutting over
  • Step 5: Run both systems in parallel for a short period with a small segment
  • Step 6: Move the remaining contacts and switch off old automations last

Keep the old account readable until you are sure every thread and consent record arrived. Opt outs in particular must carry over, since texting someone who asked to stop is the error regulators and carriers care about most.

Numbers, registration and the move

Most US business texting runs on registered ten digit numbers, toll free numbers or short codes. Each type has its own registration. When you change vendors, ask three questions up front.

  • Can my existing numbers be hosted or ported to you and how long does it take?
  • Does my current brand registration carry over or do I file again?
  • Who writes the campaign description and sample messages for the carriers?

Telnyx mentions hosted SMS on numbers you already own and SimpleTexting mentions text enabling a landline. Other vendors have their own processes. Get the answer in writing before you cancel anything.

Consent travels with the contact, not the tool

A new platform does not reset your obligations. Whoever gave permission to receive texts from you gave it to your business, so their record should move with them. The record should show when they agreed, where they agreed and the wording they saw.

We cover the rules and the permission step in detail in our B2B SMS marketing guide, so we will not repeat them here. The short version for a migration: no record, no text, until the buyer agrees again.

  • What support do we get during setup and after launch?
  • How do you charge and what fees sit outside the plan?

Ask each vendor the same questions in the same order. The differences become obvious when the answers sit side by side.

How these alternatives charge

We do not list prices, but the pricing model tells you a lot about fit. The vendors above describe a few different approaches on their own sites.

  • Usage based: Telnyx and Twilio describe paying for what you use, which suits variable product traffic
  • Pay as you go: TextMagic describes this option for teams that send irregularly
  • Volume based: TextUs describes pricing tied to volume
  • Plans: most dashboard products sell monthly or annual plans with message allowances

Carrier fees, registration fees and number costs can sit outside any of these. Ask for a full estimate based on your real volume and number types.

What B2B teams need that retail teams do not

Many texting tools grew up serving shops and restaurants. B2B use is different in ways that should shape your choice.

  • Account context: several people at one company may text you, each with separate permission
  • Long gaps: threads can pause for weeks during a deal, so history must stay attached
  • Rep ownership: buyers expect replies from the person they met, not a generic number
  • CRM reporting: success is meetings and revenue, not clicks on a coupon

Products built around CRM records or routed inboxes tend to handle these needs better than tools built for blasts. That is why Heymarket, Salesmsg, Textline and TextUs often come up in B2B shortlists.

Common mistakes when replacing a texting tool

  • Moving contacts without their consent history
  • Cancelling the old account before registration clears on the new one
  • Rebuilding every old automation instead of only the ones still used
  • Choosing on feature count rather than on the one job you need done
  • Expecting a new tool to fix slow replies when the issue is staffing

A short written plan with an owner and a cut over date prevents most of these.
