What an X ads firm should handle for a B2B team

Running X for a business buyer is more than boosting posts. Before you compare firms, agree on the parts of the job you need covered.

  • Audience building from keywords, conversation topics, accounts your buyers follow and your own lists.
  • Posts and creative written for the tone of the feed, which is quicker and more direct than LinkedIn.
  • A lead form or a landing page that asks enough to separate buyers from bystanders.
  • Tracking that ties each lead to the campaign that produced it.
  • Brand safety settings so your ads stay away from content you would not want beside them.
  • Someone who contacts each lead quickly, on your side or the firm's.

Ask every firm which of these it handles and which it expects from you. Most of the difference between proposals sits in that answer.

When X fits a B2B company and when to start elsewhere

X suits B2B companies whose buyers argue, ask and share news in public. Software, fintech, developer tools, security, policy and media are typical examples. If the people who sign your contracts follow industry voices and join threads about the problems you solve, X can put you in front of them while the topic is live.

It is a weaker first channel when your buyers rarely post or read X at all, when your offer needs a long explanation before anyone will click or when you need to reach a narrow list of named accounts by job title. In those cases LinkedIn usually comes first and X works better as a second layer that retargets people who already know you.

A quick test: search X for the phrases your buyers use about the problem. If you find active threads from people who look like your customers, X deserves a trial. If you mostly find vendors talking to each other, start somewhere else.

Targeting on X without job titles

X does not let you choose job titles or company names the way LinkedIn does. Firms that run X well for business buyers work around that with signals that point to the same people.

  • Keywords. Reach people who recently posted or searched for terms tied to your category or the problem you solve.
  • Conversation topics. Show ads to people engaging with a subject, an event or an industry discussion.
  • Follower look alikes. Reach people who resemble the followers of named accounts, such as industry publications, analysts or well known voices your buyers follow.
  • Interests. Broader categories, useful mostly as a filter on top of the signals above.

Ask each firm which accounts and keywords it would start with for your buyer. Then ask how it would check that the people clicking are the people you want. A clear answer names real accounts and phrases. A vague one names interest categories.

Your own data on X: lists, site visitors and engagers

The most reliable audiences on X are usually the ones you bring. A customer or prospect list uploaded as a tailored audience lets you reach known contacts and build look alikes from them. The X Pixel on your site lets you retarget people who visited key pages.

People who watched your video, replied to a post or visited your profile form another warm pool. For a B2B program these three sources often matter more than any cold targeting, because they start with people who have already shown interest.

Check who owns these audiences. They should sit in your own X ads account so they stay with you if the firm changes.

X ad formats a B2B team actually uses

X offers more formats than most B2B programs need. A small set does most of the work.

  • Promoted posts. A normal post shown to people who do not follow you. The workhorse for most programs.
  • Video ads. Short explainers, product walk throughs and clips from talks or webinars.
  • Carousels. A few cards in one unit, useful for showing several features or use cases.
  • Lead forms. A form inside X that collects contact details without sending the person to your site.
  • Follower ads. Grow the audience of your company or founder account so later posts reach more people for free.
  • Trend placements. Premium spots tied to trending topics. Costly, so usually saved for launches or big events.

Ask which formats a firm would start with and why. For most B2B companies a mix of promoted posts and short video, backed by retargeting, is a sensible opening.

Lead forms on X or a landing page

A lead form inside X is quick to fill in and keeps the person on the platform, which tends to raise volume. PacedRevenue's page notes that X now offers native lead forms that can ask for company name, job title and work email. The trade off is that quick forms attract some people who are only mildly curious.

Sending people to a page on your site means they leave X first. Fewer will finish, but those who do have read more about you and answered the questions you chose. You can also measure behavior on the page.

Many teams test both. Whichever you choose, decide in advance who contacts each lead and how fast. Set that response time before launch so no lead sits unanswered.

Fee models on X: flat, setup plus monthly or a cut of spend

The firms on this page that show fees use three shapes. AWDS lists starting monthly fees for management and for management plus X Ads. The B2B Playbook lists a starting monthly fee plus ad spend. Upgrow publishes a setup fee plus a monthly fee, and its X ads page adds a percentage of ad spend option, whichever is higher. HeyOz describes a flat monthly fee without a figure. Directive, Enlimited, Koozai and PacedRevenue list no fee on their X pages.

A flat fee is easy to plan around and leaves the firm no reason to inflate your budget. A percentage of spend grows with your budget, which can suit large accounts but adds up fast once a campaign works. A setup fee pays for building audiences, tracking and creative before anything runs. Whatever the shape, ask what the fee includes, since creative, landing pages, tracking and lead follow up are frequently billed on top.

Recommended spend on X

Only one firm here publishes a recommended spend range for X on its page: The B2B Playbook, at $2,000 to $3,500 a month on ads. Koozai says it sets budget from your goals instead of a fixed minimum. Enlimited describes fit by deal size rather than spend.

Whatever a firm says, X needs enough spend to learn who responds. Ask how many audiences and posts the firm would test at your budget and how long before it can tell you what is working. If your budget is far below what a firm usually manages, look for one built for smaller accounts.

Measuring X against pipeline

Platform numbers on X, such as impressions, engagements and clicks, say little about revenue on their own. A B2B program needs to know which campaigns produced leads, which of those became meetings and which became customers.

The X Pixel records actions on your site. X's Conversion API sends events from your server, which holds up better when browsers block tracking. Several firms on this page, including Enlimited, PacedRevenue and The B2B Playbook, describe connecting these to your CRM so each lead is counted against the ad that started it.

Ask who sets this up, who keeps it working and which number the firm will report each week. If the answer is engagement rate, keep asking.

Creative and tone for the X feed

X moves fast and rewards plain speech. Ads that read like a brochure get scrolled past. Ads that sound like a useful post from someone who knows the field tend to earn attention. Short lines, a clear point of view and a specific example usually work better than polished slogans.

Plan for frequent changes. People see the same post many times, so a program needs fresh posts and clips every week or two. Ask who writes them, who approves them and how quickly weak ones get switched off.

Brand safety deserves a direct question too. Ask what settings the firm uses to keep your ads away from content you would not want beside them. Ask too how it reviews placements.

How long a fair X test takes

X campaigns need time to settle. The first weeks go to finding which keywords, accounts and posts draw the right people. Early numbers move around a lot. Judging a program on its first two weeks usually leads to the wrong call.

A fair plan sets a test window up front, often around three months for a B2B offer with a long sales cycle. It names what counts as a good lead before launch, so nobody argues about it later. It also names a checkpoint each month where weak audiences and posts get cut and budget moves to what is working.

Ask each firm what it would expect to learn in month one, month two and month three at your budget. A firm that can describe those steps plainly has probably run X for business buyers before. A firm that only talks about the end result has given you less to hold it to.

Plan the follow up for the test as carefully as the ads. If leads arrive and nobody calls them for days, the test measures your response time rather than X. Decide who owns each new lead, how quickly they reply and where the outcome gets recorded.

Account ownership and contract terms

Your company should own the X ads account, the pixel, the audiences and the finished creative. The firm should work inside your account with access you can switch off. AWDS and The B2B Playbook both describe ad spend going directly to X, which usually means it comes from your account.

On terms, HeyOz describes month to month work and The B2B Playbook says it has no lock in contracts. The other firms did not state contract length on the pages we read, so ask before you sign and get any notice period in writing.

What to ask an X ads firm before you sign

  • Which keywords and accounts would you use to find our buyer on X?
  • Would you start with lead forms or a landing page for us? Why?
  • Which formats would you run in the first month?
  • How will we know which leads came from which campaign?
  • Adding fee, creative and tools together, what will we pay each month?
  • Who writes the posts and how often do they change?
  • Who contacts a new lead and how quickly?
  • Whose account do the ads run in?

Get the answers in writing. They are easier to compare across firms and harder to soften later.

Warning signs in an X ads pitch

  • Follower growth or engagement as the main goal for a sales program.
  • Interest categories as the only targeting plan.
  • No plan for the pixel, the Conversion API or lead tracking.
  • The same few posts planned for months.
  • Campaigns kept in an ad account the firm controls.
  • No one named to follow up leads.

Each of these is worth a direct question. If a firm shows several, it may be running X as if every advertiser were a consumer brand.

Where X sits next to LinkedIn and Meta

Few B2B plans lean on X by itself. LinkedIn reaches people by job title and company. Meta reaches the same people outside work and suits retargeting. X adds reach into live industry conversations and the accounts your buyers follow.

Many B2B teams use LinkedIn to find the right people, Meta to keep showing up in their feeds and X to join the conversations they care about. For a single firm that plans the three channels as one, see these B2B firms that run Meta, LinkedIn and X together.

Frequently asked questions

What does a Twitter ads agency do for a B2B company?

It plans, writes and runs paid campaigns on X for you, choosing audiences from keywords, conversations and the accounts your buyers follow, then tracks which campaigns produce leads and meetings.

How much do X ads firms charge?

Fees vary. On the pages we read, some firms publish starting monthly fees, one adds a setup fee and a percentage of spend option and several only quote after a call. Ad spend is usually paid to X on top of the fee.

Can you target B2B buyers on X without job titles?

Yes. Firms use keywords, conversation topics, look alikes of accounts your buyers follow, your own uploaded lists and people who visited your site or engaged with your posts.

Should B2B X ads use lead forms or a landing page?

Lead forms inside X are quick and raise volume. Landing pages bring fewer leads but give you room to explain and qualify. Many teams test both and decide who follows up each lead before launch.

Is X a good channel for B2B?

It can be when your buyers follow industry voices and join public threads about the problem you solve. When they rarely use X, LinkedIn or Meta usually makes a better first channel.

What does Be Leaded charge for X ads?

Programs start from $2,000 a month plus ad spend. Ad spend is paid to X directly and isn't part of our fee. We settle the exact fee with you on a 30-minute call.

Talk through X before you spend on it

Bring the buyer you want to reach, the topics they post about and any X campaigns you have run before. Expect a plain view on whether X belongs in your plan and whether we or another firm on this page should run it. Month to month, no long-term contract.
