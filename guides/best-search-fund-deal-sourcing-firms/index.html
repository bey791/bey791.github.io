The short answer for searchers

Be Leaded works with searchers who have a buy box and a limited runway but not enough owner conversations to find the one company worth buying.

Our program researches the companies inside your criteria, drafts each note with you, sends it from your name on email and LinkedIn and screens every reply, so an owner lands on your calendar only after asking to talk. Programs start from $2,000 a month. Email and LinkedIn. Calling is quoted separately. Month to month, no long-term contract. If you are comparing firms, that is our entry. The rest of this page covers what these firms actually do for a search, the fee shapes you will run into and eight firms described only from their own websites.

Which searchers this list serves

The reader we have in mind is a searcher. That covers a traditional search backed by a group of investors, a self-funded search paid for from savings and a search run by two partners. All of them are looking for one company to buy and run. All of them have a clock on the search.

It is not for an advisor or a broker who needs meetings with owners ready to sell a company on their behalf. That is the sell side of the same market. It needs a different kind of firm. Advisors can use our comparison of M&A deal sourcing firms for advisors instead.

It also assumes you already know roughly what you want to buy. If the buy box is still loose, some firms below will help you tighten it first. That is worth knowing before you compare prices.

What a sourcing firm does for a search

A sourcing firm takes the part of the search that eats the most hours: building a list of companies that fit, finding the owner of each one, writing to them, following up and sorting the replies. Done alone, that work competes with fundraising, diligence on live deals and the owner calls themselves.

The firm does not value the business, negotiate the letter of intent or arrange the loan. It does not decide whether a company is worth buying. Those stay with you. A good firm makes it easier to spend your weeks on owners who said yes to a conversation and less on spreadsheets.

Some firms stop at the list. Some stop at the first reply. Some book the call and sit in on the first conversation. Knowing where each one stops is the first thing to check.

Why sourcing for a search is its own job

A search buys one company and then the searcher runs it. That changes the outreach. The owner is not just selling to a buyer. They are deciding who will take over the staff and customers they built up over decades. The note has to come from a named person they can look up, with a clear reason for writing to this company.

A search also has limited money and time. A firm that needs a long ramp or a long minimum term uses up runway that a searcher cannot get back. A firm that sends huge volumes in a generic voice can burn through a small regional market quickly and leave owners who have already said no.

Finally, the searcher's own name matters more than in most buyer outreach. Owners often agree to talk because a person wrote to them, not because a company did. Ask every firm whose name is on the email.

Fee shapes you will see

Firms in this market charge in a handful of ways. The shape matters as much as the number, because it decides what the firm is paid to do.

  • Flat monthly fee. You pay for a defined amount of research and outreach each month. Easy to budget. The firm is paid for activity, so ask what a normal month delivers.
  • Monthly fee plus a success fee. A lower monthly fee with a payment at closing. Check how the success fee is defined and whether it applies to companies you found yourself.
  • Per qualified introduction. You pay for each owner who agrees to talk and fits your criteria, often in prepaid batches. Read the definition of a qualified owner closely.
  • Buyer paid matching. The firm introduces owners from its network and is paid by the buyer, usually only when a deal moves forward. Ask whether other buyers see the same companies.
  • Minimum terms. Some firms ask for a few months up front. That can be fair for a real test, but it should match your runway.

Several firms below publish their prices on their own sites. We describe the fee shape here and link to the source rather than repeating their figures.

How we chose and described these firms

Each firm below says on its own website that it works with searchers or search funds. We opened each site on November 19, 2026 and described each one from that site alone, linking the page we used. We did not use reviews, third party lists or claims of results.

Firms are listed A to Z by name. This order is not a ranking. A firm being on the list is not an endorsement. A firm missing from it is not a judgment. It simply means its own site did not describe search fund work clearly enough for us to cite.

Axia Growth

Axia Growth's homepage presents Axia as a lower middle market deal sourcing firm running buy side retained search and lists search funds first among the buyers it serves. It says each buyer campaign is exclusive to that buy box and that no other buyer gets the list.

The site says Axia reaches owners by email and phone under the Axia name. It says calls are made by named, US based callers it selects, trains and supervises. It says a qualified meeting means an owner inside your buy box on your calendar with revenue verified and intent confirmed and that you do not pay for a meeting that misses that bar. Pricing is not published on the homepage.

Ask Axia how owners react to a call from the firm rather than from you and how a search is introduced once the owner agrees to talk.

Be Leaded

Be Leaded runs owner outreach for searchers on email and LinkedIn, sent from the searcher's own name and approved by the searcher before anything goes out. We build the list against your criteria, screen every reply and book only the owners who asked for a call. Phone outreach is quoted separately.

Programs start from $2,000 a month. Email and LinkedIn. Calling is quoted separately. You pay a published starting price each month rather than a fee at closing. Month to month, no long-term contract. The contact file and every reply thread stay yours.

Caprae Capital

Caprae Capital's Search as a Service page describes an outsourced deal sourcing program for traditional and self-funded searchers among other buyers. It says the program builds target lists from your buy box and contacts owners through calls and email, on LinkedIn and through handwritten letters before passing on qualified owner conversations.

The page describes weekly reporting on volume, connect rates and qualified conversations. It publishes a starting monthly price with a success fee on top. The page says campaigns are set up around your own criteria for industry, size and location.

If you talk to Caprae, ask how the success fee is defined and whose name appears on each letter and call.

CAPTARGET

CAPTARGET's homepage has a section for search funds. It says the firm helps searchers build qualified pipelines quickly, with owner list building, outreach and coverage of brokers and bankers who have deals that fit the thesis.

The site describes flat monthly fees with no success fees. It says outreach runs under your alias and that you keep the alias, the owner targets, the messaging and the campaign data. Its FAQ says it does not commit to a number of leads or closed deals and publishes ranges instead. CAPTARGET is now part of SourceCo. SourceCo's own site sends search funds to CapTarget.

Ask how many owners are added in a normal month for a small regional buy box and how broker coverage fits a search that wants proprietary deals.

DealSource Systems

DealSource Systems' homepage says search funds run the same buy side program as its other buyers: a defined thesis, the owners inside it contacted directly and qualified conversations on your calendar. The site says it is based in Aarhus, Denmark. It says it covers the United States, the United Kingdom and Europe.

It publishes a monthly starting price for one thesis with a minimum term and no success fee. It says you receive the full mapped list of companies that fit your thesis as an asset you own. It describes the firm as the deal origination practice of Danish Lead Co.

For a searcher, the minimum term is the key thing to check against your runway.

Konverrt

Konverrt's homepage lists search funds among the buyers it works for and describes owner introductions sold in prepaid batches of ten. It says each lead is an owner who replied that they are open to talking and fits your criteria, checked before it is forwarded. It says any lead it misses within the batch window is refunded or credited.

The site says you approve every email and that outreach goes out from Konverrt's own aged domains, with a dedicated set under your name available as a one time setup. Its pricing page publishes a per lead range and says there is no retainer or setup fee.

Ask how owners see the sender when mail goes from Konverrt's domains and what happens after the introduction is forwarded.

OutSearched

OutSearched's search funds page describes direct to owner outreach for searchers. It lays out four stages: tightening the buy box, mapping the market, running owner outreach and qualifying fit before anything reaches the searcher. It says the firm takes a limited number of search mandates.

The page recommends its Fractional Business Development service for most searches and Buy-Side M&A Advisory when the thesis still needs work. It says first time searchers are a large share of its work. Pricing is not published on that page.

Ask whether outreach goes out under your name or theirs, which the page itself names as a question worth asking any firm.

SourceCo

SourceCo's homepage presents SourceCo as a firm for off market deal sourcing and buy side search. Its menu routes search funds to CapTarget, a SourceCo company, rather than to a separate SourceCo search program. Its FAQ names search funds among the buyers that hire it to find businesses matching a thesis.

The FAQ says SourceCo typically does not charge retainers unless a client engages it for a focused search and that owners never pay. It also describes a feed of anonymous profiles of businesses that are not listed for sale.

For a searcher, the practical question is whether you would work with SourceCo directly or through CapTarget and whether other buyers can see the same companies.

Questions to ask before you spend search capital

  • Whose name is on every email, letter and call?
  • Will I sign off on each message before sending starts?
  • Where does your list come from and do I keep it if we stop?
  • What exactly counts as a qualified owner or a qualified meeting?
  • How many owners in my buy box do you expect to contact in a normal month?
  • Is there a success fee and does it apply to companies I found myself?
  • How long is the minimum term and how much notice do I give to stop?
  • Do other buyers see the same companies you send me?
  • Who sits in on the first owner call, if anyone?

Ask the same questions of every firm and write down the answers. The differences show up quickly when the answers sit side by side.

Explaining the spend to your investors

In a traditional search, the money for sourcing comes out of a budget your investors approved. They will want to know what it buys. Before you sign, write a short note that says which firm you chose, what fee shape it uses, how many months you are committing to and what you expect to see by the first review.

Investors tend to ask the same few things: whether you still control the owner relationship, whether the firm could send the same companies to other buyers and how a success fee would affect the price of a deal. Having answers ready makes the approval quicker and protects you if the firm underdelivers.

Self-funded searchers have no one to report to, but the same note is still useful. It forces you to set a review date and a result that would justify another month.

Signs a firm is the wrong fit for your search

  • It cannot tell you whose name goes on the outreach.
  • It will not show you sample messages before you sign.
  • It counts every reply as a lead, including brokers and polite no answers.
  • Its minimum term would use up a large share of your remaining runway.
  • It describes the same companies being offered to many buyers but calls them proprietary.
  • It cannot work in your size band or your region.

Any one of these is worth a direct question. Two or three together usually mean you should keep looking.

Running a firm alongside your own outreach

Hiring a firm does not mean you stop writing to owners yourself. Many searchers keep a short list of companies they found through their own network and handle those personally, while the firm covers the wider list. Tell the firm which companies are yours so nobody writes to the same owner twice.

Broker listings and intermediaries still have a place too. A firm working proprietary outreach and a searcher watching listings can cover different parts of the same market. What matters is a single record of who has been contacted, when and what they said.

How to judge the first ninety days

The first month is mostly setup: the buy box agreed, the list built and checked, the first messages approved and sending started. Few conversations come in that month. That is normal.

By the second month you should see replies and a clear sorting of them: interested, not now, no and wrong fit. By the third month you should be able to say how many owner conversations the work produced, how many fit the buy box and whether any are worth a second meeting. If you cannot say that, ask the firm why before you renew.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best search fund deal sourcing firms?

No single firm suits every search. Firms that describe search fund work on their own sites include Axia Growth, Be Leaded, Caprae Capital, CAPTARGET, DealSource Systems, Konverrt, OutSearched and SourceCo. Compare whose name is on the outreach, the fee shape, the minimum term and what counts as a qualified owner.

What does a search fund deal sourcing firm do?

It builds a list of companies that fit the searcher's buy box, finds and contacts the owners, follows up and sorts the replies so the searcher spends time on owners who agreed to talk. Valuation, negotiation and financing stay with the searcher.

Is Be Leaded a deal sourcing firm for search funds?

Yes. Be Leaded runs owner outreach for searchers on email and LinkedIn, sent from the searcher's own name, with every reply screened before a call is booked.

How much does Be Leaded charge searchers?

Programs start from $2,000 a month. Email and LinkedIn. Calling is quoted separately. We confirm the exact figure on a 30-minute call.

Do searchers sign a long contract?

Programs run month to month with no long-term contract. The contact file and reply threads stay yours.

Should a searcher pay a success fee for sourcing?

It depends on your budget and your investors. A success fee lowers the monthly cost but adds a payment at closing. Check how it is defined and whether it applies to companies you found yourself.

Do self-funded searchers use sourcing firms?

Yes. Several firms say on their own sites that they work with self-funded searchers. Runway and minimum terms matter even more when the search is paid for personally.

Can I keep writing to owners myself while a firm runs outreach?

Yes. Keep a shared record of who is contacted by whom, so no owner hears from two people about the same search.
