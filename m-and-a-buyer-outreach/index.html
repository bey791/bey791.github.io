Buyer outreach for business brokers

Buyer outreach finds strategic acquirers, search funds and PE groups that fit a listing you already hold, then contacts them under a blind note and an NDA. Be Leaded runs the research, notes you approve and meetings with buyers who asked for a look under NDA.

What buyer outreach covers, from signed listing to first buyer call

The work starts once a seller has signed with your firm and ends when a qualified buyer is on your calendar. Between those two points sit four jobs: deciding who could buy, finding the right person at each buyer, writing a note that protects the seller's name and handling the replies that come back.

Listing marketplaces and the file your firm already holds reach buyers who are looking this week. Written outreach goes to the ones who are not looking. Picture an operator that has never opened a listing site but would buy a competitor in the next county if someone put it in front of them.

We run the research, the sending and the reply screening for that group. Your firm keeps the seller relationship, the NDA, the memorandum and every conversation about price and terms. We do not advise on the deal. Your team does.

Buyer types we reach

Three buyer types. Named the same way across this site.

Strategic acquirers

Operators that buy in the same industry for customers, geography or capability. The blind note speaks to the fit, not a blast list.

Search funds

Searchers with a thesis and a timeline. We match thesis to listing before a note goes out.

PE groups

Funds and platform companies hunting for add on deals that fit the size and sector of the listing.

Independent sponsors and family offices appear in our buyer education guide. They are not the targets of this outreach track. The working steps sit in how business brokers find buyers.

The buy box every note is checked against

A buy box is a short written description of the buyer a listing is meant for. Without one, a list drifts toward whoever is easiest to find. With one, every name can be checked against something your firm and the seller have already read.

What goes into the box

  • The industry, plus the neighboring industries worth reading
  • The geography, including regions a buyer might want to enter
  • Revenue and earnings in bands wide enough that they cannot identify the seller
  • What the buyer would actually be buying, such as a customer base, a territory, a crew, a license or a brand
  • What the seller will accept, such as an owner who stays through a handover or a buyer who keeps the staff
  • Buyers the seller wants kept out

Why bands beat exact figures

A precise figure can point to one company in a directory. A band does the same job for a buyer deciding whether to keep reading, and the seller's name stays out of reach.

Who signs it off

Your firm signs off on the box before research starts. It is the first point where the seller's wishes enter the process, so have the seller's view in hand when you review it.

What each buyer type needs from a first note

The three buyer types we reach read a note for different reasons. One note written for all of them wastes the only thing a first touch has, which is a reason to reply.

A strategic acquirer reads for fit

The note names the category, a region and the capability or customer base the listing would add. The reader is often someone in corporate development or the owner of a regional operator, and the question in their head is whether this fills a gap. A competitor, a supplier, a customer and a company in a neighboring region each buy for a different reason, so the sentence that explains fit changes with them.

A search fund reads for the thesis

A searcher is usually looking for one company to run. Many publish what they want, so the note should echo the box they posted instead of guessing. A listing outside a searcher's stated industry or size is a skip, not a stretch.

A PE group reads for the add on

A fund or a platform company looks for an acquisition that suits a company it already owns. Open the firm's own portfolio page, see what it holds in the niche and write about the add on, not about the seller's history.

Independent sponsors and family offices buy companies too. They sit outside this program and appear in the step by step guide to finding buyers as part of learning who buys.

Building the buyer list

A buyer list is a set of named people at named companies, each with a reason to be there. A column of company names with no contact is only a to do list.

Where the names come from

Start from the industry and its neighbors: the supplier, the customer and the complementary service. Add the public record of who has bought in the niche, such as a sponsor's portfolio page or a company's own announcement of an acquisition. Add the searchers whose posted box fits the listing.

Matching a person to a buyer

The right reader is a role. At a strategic it is corporate development or the owner. At a fund it is a principal or an operating partner. At a search fund it is the searcher. We verify every contact before the first note sends and we use no rented lists.

Putting the list in order

Not every buyer needs to hear at the same moment. Many brokers sort the list into groups: the buyers with the strongest fit, the buyers worth a try and the buyers held back until the first group has answered. Your firm sets that order, and nothing is sent until you have approved the message.

Names to keep off the list

Agree an exclusion list before anything is sent. It usually holds the seller's direct competitors when the seller wants them kept in the dark, customers and suppliers whose reaction matters and any party your firm has a reason to avoid. Ask any provider to show you how that list is applied.

How buyer outreach works

From your listing brief to a buyer meeting on your calendar.

  1. Week 1

    Brief and buy box

    Industry, geography, revenue band, margin profile and the deal thesis. We turn that into a buy box you sign off on before research starts.

  2. Week 2

    Buyer research

    Strategic acquirers, search funds and PE groups matched to the box. Every contact is verified. No rented lists.

  3. Week 3

    Blind outreach

    Email and LinkedIn start at a careful pace. The first note names no company. You approve every message.

  4. Ongoing

    NDA and meetings

    Replies are screened for fit. Serious buyers sign your NDA, receive the memorandum from your team and book time with you.

The blind note and the teaser

The blind note is the first message a buyer sees. It names no company, no street and no figure that points to one. The teaser is the short summary a buyer receives after replying, and your firm decides whether it goes before or after the NDA.

What a blind note holds

  • One sentence on the kind of business
  • A region broad enough to cover many possible companies
  • One reason this type of buyer would care
  • A small ask, such as a reply or an NDA
  • An easy way to say no

A note to a search fund, as an example

Subject: Home services company, owner open to talk

Priya, your posted search covers founder owned home services companies in the Midwest. I represent one in that range with a long local record. The owner is open to a conversation under NDA.

If it sits inside your box I can send a one page summary once an NDA is signed. If it does not, a single line back is enough and I will close the file.

Dana Whitlow, Corbel Advisory

This is an example. Names and details are illustrative.

What stays out of the first touch

The legal name, the street, the owner's name, customer names, any figure precise enough to match a directory entry and the memorandum. Detail waits until the buyer has signed.

How the NDA fits into buyer outreach

Buyer outreach ends at a signed NDA and a booked call, so the NDA is where the hand off happens. We do not advise on the deal, and that includes NDA terms. Your firm's own form and your firm's attorney decide what a buyer signs.

Settle these before the first note

  • Which form your firm uses and who at your firm sends it
  • Whether the seller's attorney has read it
  • Who is allowed to sign for a fund or a strategic
  • Whether terms on employees and customers are included
  • Where signed copies are kept

A buyer who wants to change the wording

Some buyers ask for edits. That conversation belongs to your firm. A buyer who would rather sign nothing before seeing the name has answered the question. Log it and move on.

After the signature

The memorandum goes out from your team. Only then does a buyer learn the seller's name, address and figures.

Follow-up that does not burn the list

Many buyers will not answer a first note. That is normal for any cold message and it is not a verdict. A second and third touch, spaced as your firm decides, give a busy corporate development team a chance to see the listing. Each one is shorter than the last and adds something new, such as a different angle on fit.

Email and LinkedIn run together. An advisor's own LinkedIn profile can carry a short message beside the email. A phone call is a separate job and is quoted separately.

Stop rules

A buyer who opts out stops hearing from us. A buyer who says the box is wrong gets one line of thanks and is marked as not a fit. A buyer who goes quiet after signing is your firm's to follow, because it has become a process question.

A buyer who says not now

That buyer stays on the file with the reason noted, so the record shows when it is worth asking again. The file stays with your firm.

Sorting replies into five piles

Replies come back in different shapes and each one needs a different next step. They are read and sorted against the buy box before anything reaches your calendar.

Ready for an NDA

The buyer fits the box and asks for more. Your firm sends the NDA and books the call.

Asking questions first

The buyer wants to know the region, the size band or the reason for the sale before signing. Answer only what the blind note allows and keep the name back.

Not now

The buyer is interested in the niche and busy with something else. Note the reason and leave the buyer alone.

Wrong fit

The listing sits outside the buyer's industry, size or geography. A short, courteous close is enough.

Opt out

The buyer asks to hear nothing further. The address is suppressed and stays suppressed.

The tests behind the sort are fit, funds and timing, and the call on funds stays with your firm. A buyer who looks right on paper and cannot close is not yet a buyer.

Running more than one listing at once

A firm with several active listings has several buy boxes. Keep each listing in its own file with its own box, its own blind note and its own exclusion list. A buyer that suits two listings should hear about them in a way that reveals neither, so decide ahead of time whether that happens in one note or two and which listing leads.

Bring the list of live listings to the first call, and say which are about to launch and which are on hold.

When a listing sells or is pulled

Tell whoever runs the outreach the same day, so no further notes go out for it. Buyers who were mid conversation get a short, courteous close from your firm.

Confidentiality questions to settle with any provider

Outreach on a quiet listing puts the seller's name at risk, so confidentiality deserves a written answer. These questions fit any provider, Be Leaded included.

Who sees the list and the replies

Replies carry what a buyer says about its interest and its appetite. A broker would normally keep that from other buyers. Ask how threads are stored and who can read them. Here the list and every reply are yours.

Who can send under your name

Each note carries your brand on the sending domain and your wording, and nothing leaves without your approval. Ask any provider whether anything can go out that your firm has not read.

What exclusive access really covers

If a provider talks about exclusive access, ask what it covers and get the answer in writing. A provider that also works for firms in your sector owes you a plain account of how the lists are kept apart.

Buyer confidentiality

A fund exploring add ons in a niche may not want that known. Treat what a buyer says as information that stays with the broker and the seller.

Questions to ask any buyer outreach provider

Use the list below with every firm you speak to. We would rather you ask all of it.

  • Who researches the buyers and how is each contact checked?
  • Who writes the notes, who approves them and which domains send them?
  • What does a blind note include and what does it leave out?
  • Who reads a reply first and what happens to it next?
  • What happens when a buyer asks to be left alone?
  • If the program ends, who keeps the list and the reply threads?
  • Which tasks stay with your firm?
  • What does the provider say it cannot do?

Our answers are on this page. Notes are yours to approve. The list and the replies belong to your firm. The NDA, the memorandum and every conversation about the deal stay with your team.

Running buyer outreach in house

A broker can run this alone. It takes someone to build the list, someone to keep sending domains healthy, someone to read replies on the day they arrive and someone with the authority to approve notes. Where those hours exist, in house is a fair choice. The broker's guide to finding buyers lays out the steps, and the notes on sending domains and authentication cover the technical side.

Where the brokers are tied up with sellers and no one is responsible for the buyer file, a provider can handle buyer research, mailbox setup and reading replies. The firm keeps approvals, the NDA and the conversations.

Where buyer outreach stops

It cannot create a buyer for a listing that does not fit its market, and it cannot make a buyer pay a given price. A buyer that exists and fits can be put in front of the listing. The decision stays with the buyer. We do not invent a buyer or a waiting process, and we do not advise on value, terms or the deal.

A listing priced well above what comparable companies have drawn will bring back replies that say so. That feedback is useful, and weighing it is a job for your firm and the seller.

Getting ready for the first call

The first call is thirty minutes with the founder. The more of the list below you bring, the more concrete it gets, and a rough version of each item is fine.

  • The listing brief, in whatever shape it exists
  • A draft buy box, or the notes you would use to write one
  • Buyers your firm has already spoken to
  • Names the seller wants kept off the list
  • Who approves notes and who sends the NDA
  • Whether a teaser and a memorandum already exist

If the memorandum is ready and the buyer file is thin, this is the track. If you do not hold a listing yet, the owner outreach program is the better place to start.

Questions about buyer outreach

Ask anything else on the call.

What is buyer outreach for business brokers?

Buyer outreach for business brokers means we contact the strategic acquirers, search funds and PE groups most likely to buy a listing you already hold. Be Leaded builds the buyer list to each deal, writes the blind note in your firm's name and books meetings with buyers who ask for a memorandum under NDA.

What does a buyer outreach provider actually do?

It researches the buyers that fit a listing, finds the right person at each one, writes the blind note, sends it from your firm's name and reads the replies. Your firm approves the notes and takes over once a buyer is ready for an NDA.

When should a broker start buyer outreach on a listing?

After the seller has signed with your firm and the buy box is agreed. A draft summary of the business helps, and a memorandum that is ready to send after the NDA helps more.

Does buyer outreach replace listing marketplaces?

No. A marketplace reaches buyers who are browsing and written outreach reaches buyers who are not. A listing can use both, as long as the seller is comfortable with how visible a marketplace listing makes the business.

How is a buy box different from a buyer list?

The buy box describes the buyer: industry, region, size band, what they would be buying and what the seller will accept. The buyer list is the set of named people who match it. The box comes first and the list is checked against it.

Which buyer types do you reach?

Strategic acquirers, search funds and PE groups. Those are the three buyer types this program is built for. Independent sponsors and family offices show up in market education, but they are not the targets of this track.

Do you send in our firm's name?

Yes. Sending domains are tied to your brand and the copy is in your voice. You approve every message before it sends, and meetings land on your brokers' or advisors' calendars.

How do you keep a listing confidential?

The first note is blind. No company name, no address, no financials. A buyer who wants more signs your NDA before a memorandum goes out. We never publish owner or listing names, and we never name a client without your approval.

Do you contact the seller's competitors?

Only if the buy box includes them and you approve it. Many sellers want competitors left out, so put those names on the exclusion list before research starts.

Do you contact individual buyers?

No. The program is built for strategic acquirers, search funds and PE groups. Individual buyers, independent sponsors and family offices come through other routes, such as listing marketplaces and your own buyer file.

Who sends the NDA?

Your firm. It uses its own form, and your attorney decides the terms. We book the call and your team handles the paperwork.

Do you give advice on NDA terms or on the deal?

No. We do not advise on the deal, and that includes NDA wording, value and terms. Those stay with your team and the seller's advisors.

What happens when a buyer replies with questions before signing?

The reply is sorted against the buy box. Answers stay inside what the blind note allows, so the name, the street and the financials wait for the NDA.

Can you run outreach for several listings at once?

Bring the list of live listings to the first call. Each one needs its own buy box and its own exclusion list so a buyer that fits two listings hears about each in the way you decide.

What if my own buyer file already has names?

Share the file on the first call so the buy box and the exclusion list reflect who your firm has already spoken to. Anyone you want your own team to contact belongs on the exclusion list.

What happens when a buyer opts out?

The buyer stops hearing from us and the address is suppressed.

How does this sit next to seller outreach?

Seller outreach finds owners who may sell. Buyer outreach finds buyers for listings you already have. The two tracks sit beside each other. Read the seller program at off market deal flow for M&A advisors and business brokers.

How fast does a program go live?

Most programs go live in about three weeks. Inboxes warm up first, and nothing sends until you sign off on the opening sequence.

Who owns the list and the replies?

You own the list and every reply. When the program stops, the contacts and the threads stay with you.

What does buyer outreach cost?

Email and LinkedIn. Calling is quoted separately. The number depends on how many listings you bring and the channels we run, and you will have it after a thirty minute call.

Where does buyer outreach stop?

It cannot create a buyer for a listing that does not fit its market, and it cannot make a buyer pay a given price. We do not advise on value, terms or the deal.

What should we have ready for the first call?

The listing brief, a draft buy box, the buyers your firm has already spoken to, the names the seller wants kept off the list and the name of whoever approves notes and sends the NDA.
